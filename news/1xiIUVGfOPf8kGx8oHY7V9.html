Как Сфера стала сердцем Гран-при Лас-Вегаса

It's eye-catching, spectacular and unmistakably Las Vegas. The Sphere has become an integral part of the Las Vegas Grand Prix, and we explore how and why.

From giant driver portraits to live onboard footage and race updates, the Sphere has become one of the defining sights of the Las Vegas Grand Prix.

Having opened just weeks before Formula 1 returned to the city in 2023, the enormous entertainment venue quickly became part of the race's identity, with its illuminated exterior providing a spectacular backdrop to the action.

But the Sphere is far more than a giant advertising screen. With its ability to display everything from yellow flags to live footage, its become a staple of the race with increasingly creative ways to showcase information.

What is the Sphere?

The Sphere opened on September 29, 2023, with Irish rock band U2 beginning a 40-show residency at the venue. Its distinctive design and enormous LED exterior immediately made it one of Las Vegas' most recognisable landmarks.

Made up of 54,000 metres squared of LED displays on its exterior, it is the largest video screen in the world, and inside, an audience of up to 20,000 people are treated to a 16K resolution wraparound interior LED screen.

Formula 1 returned to Las Vegas on November 18, 2023, more than four decades after the city hosted the Caesars Palace Grand Prix in 1981 and 1982. With the Sphere sitting alongside the new circuit, it quickly became part of the event's identity, entertaining fans while providing live updates from the track.

How F1 embraced the Sphere

Right from the off, the Sphere would sync with the FIA’s circuit calls, displaying a huge yellow flag and a wrap-around Safety Car message, or a rippling red flag if the session had come to a stop.

Orbi, the giant yellow emoji face displayed on the Sphere, even donned a racing helmet and appeared to watch the cars as they exited Turn 9. Driver profiles were also on a loop, helping educate fans about what the drivers looked like outside of the cockpit and which teams they drove for.

And since then, the use of the huge display that measures 112m high and 157m wide has got ever more creative. Onboards were a frequent feature during the 2025 event, showing the cars lapping this rapid 6.201km track layout.

"The Sphere has become a great partner, and so we have a lot of advanced conversations and meetings to organise the different types of content,” Austin Brown, VP of Marketing at the Las Vegas Grand Prix, explains.

"We have the partnerships content as well as chequered flags, yellow and red flags, and then we have some podium celebrations, so it's a combination of F1's broadcast team cueing content in real time with the Sphere team who are on site for the duration of the week.

"We actually pull some of the telemetry data from the feed that goes over to the Sphere team, so that we can track things like fastest lap; depending on how the race results end up, we can post that content live to Sphere. So we pre-build a lot of the content that's driver- or team related, and some of these moments that we know might occur on track, and then we feed that data or the broadcast team cues live content to align with track activity for broadcast, socials or the on-site experience with fans in attendance.”

Where sport, entertainment and technology mix

The Las Vegas Grand Prix has proved to be the pinnacle of F1’s entertainment ecosystem, with continuous celebrity spotting, big shows and performances across the race weekend, and definitely follows the saying “everything is bigger in Vegas”. For the race organisers, the Sphere was going to be no exception.

“Las Vegas' role on the F1 calendar is to push the sports and entertainment landscape,” Brown says, describing the Sphere as “an iconic canvas” that is “a massive win for everybody involved".

As a Las Vegas native, Brown loves to see the tourist attractions showcased on the Sphere, and adds that the vibrancy of its neon colours fits a night race very well.

"To see a lot of the F1 drivers at that scale on Sphere is just a really fun opportunity. We've pre-filmed content with drivers in the past where they're taking different actions on the Sphere; there's an opportunity for animated and still graphics up there. To see Lewis Hamilton at the scale of Sphere is just a really special moment to deliver to fans. We're excited to see what kind of content is produced and live this year.”

Bringing the Las Vegas experience to F1

Inside the Sphere, the schedule is packed. After this year’s Las Vegas Grand Prix, Backstreet Boys will be performing the Official Afterparty at Sphere. They’re one of several groups and artists who have had residencies at the unique venue, with others including the likes of Metallica and No Doubt, and more are scheduled throughout 2027, with John Mayer and P!nk both booked in.

As well as pop royalty, there are also many immersive shows to watch inside such as The Wizard of Oz which comes with 4D effects. The grand space of the venue, which can fit the entire Statue of Liberty inside, has 17,385 seats and uses Stereographic Projection and custom cameras to capture the ultra-wide videos and images that you see on the wraparound screens.

“I think when Liberty Media acquired the sport, and then F1 was looking at new markets back in 2022, that is when they really started to explore Vegas as a market,” Brown concludes. “I think it was really trying to find a platform to continue to grow the sport to a lot of untapped fans in the US. So where better to do that than in the sports and entertainment capital of the world with a night race that just shows off everything the city has to offer?

“We've worked really hard to try and deliver what the city offers in the way of entertainment, food, beverage, and hospitality, and pull that onto track so that it's one cohesive Vegas experience."

What began as an eye-catching backdrop to F1's return to Las Vegas has become something much more. Three years on, the Sphere is firmly established as part of the Grand Prix experience, bringing the action on track to life on a scale unlike anywhere else on the calendar.
