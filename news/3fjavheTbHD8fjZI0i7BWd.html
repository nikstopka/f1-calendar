5 сюжетных линий, которые мы с нетерпением ждем перед Гран-при Лас-Вегаса

Chris Medland runs through the biggest talking points as F1 heads to Las Vegas for Round 21 of the 2026 season.

20 races down, three to go. The 2026 Formula 1 season is set to kick off the final triple-header of the year, and even with the winners of both championships already decided, there are plenty of storylines to get excited about.

Here are just a few of the talking points as the paddock heads to Las Vegas.

Lawson and Lindblad stay

Across the previous triple-header in Austin, Mexico City and Sao Paulo, there was a growing focus on the Racing Bulls driver line-up for 2027.

Arvid Lindblad’s impressive rookie season always looked likely to secure him a second year, as the teenager continues his development and looks to build on multiple point-scoring finishes. However, the other side of the garage appeared to have less certainty over what would happen next.

Liam Lawson has enjoyed an outstanding season, including a very solid three-race run at Red Bull in place of the injured Isack Hadjar at the end of August and early September. The New Zealander has also led the field outside the top four teams on numerous occasions and continued that form even when he didn’t have confirmation of a future seat.

With Racing Bulls weighing up whether to promote the talented Nikola Tsolov into a race seat in 2027, Lawson produced a run of four top seven finishes in five races, finishing ninth behind the two Mercedes, Ferrari, Red Bull and McLaren drivers in the middle of that stretch in Austin.

Alan Permane’s team confirmed the current pairing of Lawson and Lindblad will continue into 2027 last week, and now the focus will be on the progress Red Bull want to see from each moving forward.

Cadillac’s driver decision

It isn’t just at Racing Bulls where there has been uncertainty over a driver line-up, with Cadillac also considering their options.

Sergio Perez is already committed on a long-term contract, with an extension being announced in Mexico City as a reward for both his strong performances on track but also the work that Perez has been doing to try and push the team forward behind the scenes.

His team mate Valtteri Bottas was also signed for his experience to help develop Cadillac, but there were questions from the team regarding some of his performances during the year, and there was a clear replacement option being explored in the form of Colton Herta.

The American former IndyCar star received a number of extra FP1 outings in order to help him secure the points required to apply for his FIA Super Licence, and duly reached that target last time out at Interlagos.

That gave Cadillac the ability to weigh up their options over whether they want to stick with Bottas - who is under contract - or give Herta his first opportunity to race in F1.

Ahead of another home race for Cadillac, a decision is understood to have been made, with the expectation that the team will continue with their current experienced pairing into 2027, and an announcement due during the Las Vegas Grand Prix week.

Camara gets another outing

There are so many driver stories to keep an eye on at the moment, and another is more immediate than the two above as Haas adapt to life after Esteban Ocon.

The Frenchman was replaced in the middle of the Mexico City Grand Prix race weekend by Ryo Hirakawa, and the experienced reserve driver duly earned himself a point on debut as he was able to take advantage of post-race penalties for others.

With Hirakawa racing in the World Endurance Championship at Monza the following weekend, Haas then took the opportunity to give their 2027 race driver Rafael Camara an early debut, with the Brazilian taking the seat for the Sao Paulo Grand Prix.

At the time, Haas made clear the decisions only related to those specific race weekends, and there had been no final call on who would drive for the remainder of the season. Theoretically, Haas could alternate between both Camara and Hirakawa, although they can’t turn to another option without requiring special dispensation from the FIA due to a rule limiting teams to four race drivers per season.

Haas have now confirmed Camara will retain the drive for the rest of the year as they get a head start on 2027, with the 21-year-old building on his first experience during a challenging Interlagos weekend.

Championship battles for drivers and teams

Mercedes wrapped up the Teams’ Championship at the last race in Brazil, one week on from Kimi Antonelli becoming the youngest Drivers’ Champion in history in Mexico. But that doesn’t mean there isn’t still plenty to play for.

From a teams’ perspective, so much of the focus is on who wins the title, but the prize money is paid out based on where each team finishes in the constructors’ standings, meaning each position below P1 still carries a huge amount of weight.

Ferrari look well placed to wrap up second place overall, but behind that is a close battle for third between Red Bull and McLaren. The double podium at Interlagos allowed Red Bull to pull 17 points clear of McLaren, but it was Lando Norris who took victory in a sign of McLaren’s potential after introducing an upgrade in Brazil.

McLaren Team Principal Andrea Stella says there will be further additions in Las Vegas as they continue developing with 2027 in mind, and they could well help McLaren stay in touch with Red Bull entering the final rounds.

When it comes to the drivers’ standings, it’s more a case of pride being at stake, but Max Verstappen currently occupies second place behind Antonelli after a remarkable run of eight straight podiums - including three victories.

Verstappen is looking to extend his current tally of seven consecutive seasons finishing in the top three in the championship - an eighth would match Lewis Hamilton’s record - but there are just 33 points covering the Dutchman, George Russell, Hamilton, Charles Leclerc and Norris, with all five having won races this year.

A spectacular racing venue

We have seen all sorts of action in Las Vegas since the race joined the calendar in 2023, with last lap overtakes, wet qualifying sessions and reliability issues all playing a role in producing drama at different phases of the weekend.

The Las Vegas Strip Circuit is a unique challenge for multiple reasons, not least the near-two-kilometre-long run along the Strip that sees the cars flashing past a number of the most iconic resorts in the city.

Racing under the lights means competitive track action taking place after dark as temperatures drop, and that means it is often the coldest race weekend of the year as well.

With teams having to juggle energy demands on a circuit with multiple long straights, it could well be another race weekend with plenty of overtaking right up until the final lap.
