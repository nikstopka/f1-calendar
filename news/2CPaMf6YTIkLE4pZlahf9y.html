McLaren полон решимости бороться за третье место «до самого конца»

On the latest episode of F1 Nation, Racing Director Randy Singh opened up on the pressure McLaren are under to clinch P3 in the Teams’ Championship.

Racing Director Randy Singh is optimistic that McLaren can maximise their performance in the final three rounds and secure third place in the Teams’ Championship, but noted that they don’t “feel any less pressure than the last couple of season finales”.

After clinching back-to-back constructors’ titles in 2024 and 2025, McLaren slipped behind Mercedes and Ferrari in this season’s pecking order, and lost an additional spot to Red Bull during the recent triple header.

However, they received a crucial boost as Lando Norris made the most of their upgrades at Interlagos to take his third victory of the campaign, which could provide the momentum they need to overhaul Red Bull’s 17-point advantage in the standings.

Asked on the latest episode of F1 Nation whether they can close the gap, Singh replied: “We'll do our best to make sure it goes down to the wire and in our favour. We're treating it like a mini championship on our side, so we're just aiming to do our best at each race.

“I think it’s what saw us through both 2024/2025 Abu Dhabi races – there was a lot of pressure. We just try to focus on doing the best that we can in each area, and the result will take care of itself, hopefully.

“It's interesting because it doesn’t feel [like] any less pressure than the last couple of season finales to be honest. We've got three races as well, which is quite nice because we've got plenty of time to try and harvest as many points as we can.

“Everyone's excited and then we get straight into Las Vegas preparation as well. And this week everyone's pretty intense with seeing what we can learn from the last race – both in the dry and in the mixed conditions – and what we can do to prepare for Vegas. So, full activity and steam ahead.”

Since Las Vegas was added to the calendar in 2023, it’s become one of McLaren’s weaker tracks, with costly errors, a lack of pace and even a double disqualification limiting them to a total of just 17 points over three events.

They’re hoping to turn that form around when the paddock heads back to The Strip this week, especially as they have another chance to capitalise on the effective upgrades they introduced in Brazil.

“We all see it as a challenge and a big opportunity for us to really go and get a result at a circuit that we haven't had a good result at yet,” Singh explained. “And so we're all kind of approaching it with that kind of relish.

“We had some good upgrades on the car in Brazil, so we've got some work to do to still keep getting the most out of those. We're really approaching it from that underdog sense of, ‘we've not had a good one there, but we've got a good chance to have a really good result there [this year].

“The upgrades worked well – you can tell that also from Oscar [Piastri], who only had them from FP2 onwards because we missed FP1 in Brazil. Both drivers really quickly got up to speed again, especially Oscar. I think missing an FP1 session is tricky.

“We had the car in a good window in Brazil. We need to try and make sure we do the same in Vegas. It'll be much colder, I think, than it was in Brazil. The grip can be very low. Circuit characteristics are completely different as well.

“So we've got a lot of work to do to try and make sure that we hit the ground running, and that we keep optimising the package as it is now. It's not easy with these cars.

“Whilst we've had these cars now for the best part of a year, the upgrades, as Andrea [Stella] said before, are really quite significant. They changed the way the car behaves and we have to kind of re-learn some of that stuff each time.”

McLaren’s battle for P3 in the Constructors’ Championship was just one of the talking points that emerged in the Las Vegas Grand Prix preview, which saw Jolyon Palmer and James Hinchcliffe join F1 Nation host Tom Clarkson.
