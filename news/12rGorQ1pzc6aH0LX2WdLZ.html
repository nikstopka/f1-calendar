FIA подтверждает незначительное превышение лимита расходов Williams на 2025 год

The FIA have completed their review of the F1 teams' and power unit manufacturers' compliance with the 2025 Financial Regulations.

Formula 1's governing body the FIA have completed their review of the F1 teams’ and power unit manufacturers’ compliance with the 2025 Financial Regulations, with Williams found to have committed an unintentional minor overspend breach and a procedural breach, while Alpine also made a procedural breach.

As part of the review by the Cost Cap Administration (CCA) – a process that took eight months – all 11 Formula 1 teams and all five power unit (PU) manufacturers submitted financial documentation from 2025.

Nine of the 11 teams and all five power unit manufacturers were found in compliance for 2025, but Williams were found to have unintentionally committed a minor overspend breach and a procedural breach.

In a document confirming that an Accepted Breach Agreement (ABA) has been entered into between Williams and the Cost Cap Administration, it was explained that “there is no evidence that WGPE [Williams Grand Prix Engineering] gained or sought to gain any advantage (sporting or otherwise) from the identified breaches”, and that “the minor overspend breach amounts to £627k and therefore, with 0.48% over the 2025 Cost Cap, falls at the lower end of the <5% minor overspend range”.

Williams will subsequently pay a financial penalty for the breach, as well as taking a 1.5% reduction in wind tunnel and CFD testing over the next 12 months.

The team said in a statement: “The breach relates to administrative costs we incurred making changes to our company structure. We believed these costs fell outside of the cap. Following discussions with the FIA, we have agreed to resolve the matter in line with their interpretation that the costs should have been in cap.

“The FIA has noted that Williams did not gain any sporting or financial advantage, and nor did we ever seek to. It has also noted that we acted cooperatively and in good faith throughout the review process.

“Integrity is central to what Williams stands for and we can now close this case and remain focused on our future. We have always been and will continue to be strong supporters of the Cost Cap, which naturally requires accurate policing to be effective.”

A procedural breach was identified for Alpine, with the FIA confirming that “there is no evidence that Alpine gained or sought to gain any advantage from the inaccurate exclusions or adjustments of certain costs; and notwithstanding the Cost Cap Administration’s determination of procedural breach, Alpine’s relevant costs in respect of the 2025 Reporting Period were below the 2025 Cost Cap”.

An Accepted Breach Agreement has been entered into between the team and the Cost Cap Administration, with Alpine set to pay a financial penalty and bear the costs incurred by the Cost Cap Administration in connection with its investigation of the Procedural Breach and the preparation of the ABA.
