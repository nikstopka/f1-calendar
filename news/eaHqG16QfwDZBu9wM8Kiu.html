Formula 1 is preparing for a potential celebration on Sunday in Mexico City as Kimi Antonelli stands on the brink of being named the sport’s youngest-ever World Champion at the Autodromo Hermanos Rodriguez.

However, the young Italian is not the favourite in the betting markets to clinch his historic title win with a victory in the Mexico City Grand Prix after qualifying fifth during Qualifying.

He will start from the second row in fourth after being the beneficiary of a one-place grid penalty handed to Max Verstappen for his clash with Liam Lawson during Free Practice on Friday.

The Mercedes driver is joint-fourth favourite to win on Sunday at odds of 11/2 (6.50, +550), which would be his 10th win of the season following an impressive victory in Texas last week.

It will be Lewis Hamilton starting on pole for the first time for Ferrari after going quickest by just 0.009 seconds from George Russell in Q3.

The seven-time World Champion is the favourite to convert pole into victory in Mexico City, with odds available at 11/8 (2.38, +138) for his first Grand Prix win since the Barcelona-Catalunya Grand Prix in June.

Mexico City Grand Prix Bet Builder

1. Charles Leclerc top-two finish 2. Max Verstappen podium finish 3. Lando Norris top-six finish 4. Arvid Lindblad points finish

Leg 1: Charles Leclerc top-two finish

Ferrari took their first Grand Prix pole position of the season on Saturday but it was something of a surprise that Hamilton was the driver to achieve it as Charles Leclerc had been given a better chance of going fastest by bookmakers.

Leclerc had been the second-favourite in Qualifying behind Verstappen after setting the fastest time in FP1 and FP2 and he was also ahead of Hamilton in FP3, setting the third-best time while the British driver was sixth.

Hamilton was 0.119 seconds faster when it mattered most in Q3, but Leclerc still looks set for a strong Sunday and can be fancied to edge forward from third into the top two.

Leg 2: Max Verstappen podium finish

Verstappen was a beaten favourite in Qualifying as he finished fourth, and he will have to start fifth after his Friday collision with his former Red Bull team mate Lawson, but he should still be in the running to extend his run of six consecutive podium finishes.

The Dutch driver is a five-time winner at this track and, having been just 0.059 seconds behind Leclerc in Qualifying, he should be competitive again.

With two Ferraris and two Mercedes ahead of him, it will be an action-packed fight at the front and nobody would be surprised to see Verstappen firmly in the mix.

Leg 3: Lando Norris top-six finish

Lando Norris suffered another disappointing drop in Q3 after looking competitive in the early exchanges, but can still make his mark in the race on Sunday to maintain the top-six position he will have at the start.

The McLaren driver set the fastest time in Q2 and while he claimed that was a "magical lap" that he knew it would be hard to repeat, the car clearly has the pace to finish in the top six.

The British driver has finished lower than sixth in only three of the races that he has completed this season and he was also the winner in Mexico City last year.

Leg 4: Arvid Lindblad points finish

Arvid Lindblad will be carrying the hopes of his Racing Bulls team on Sunday after Lawson was disqualified from Qualifying for a technical breach, with the rookie aiming to add to the team's points tally in Mexico.

Lindblad will start in eighth and, while the McLaren of Oscar Piastri is an obvious danger to him in 12th, the Australian has to get past both Alpines and Nico Hulkenberg's Audi to challenge him.

The young Briton has not scored at the last two races, but has been in the points at 10 of the past 11 races and can get back in the points this week.
