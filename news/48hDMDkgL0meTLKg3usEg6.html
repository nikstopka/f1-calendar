Alpine разочарована, несмотря на «идеальный» круг Гасли в Q2

Pierre Gasly and Franco Colapinto were left disappointed by a messy Q3 session at the Las Vegas Grand Prix, having shown encouraging pace for Alpine earlier in qualifying.

A Las Vegas GP Qualifying session that briefly promised much more ended in disappointment for Alpine as Pierre Gasly and Franco Colapinto finished at the bottom of Q3 in P9 and P10.

Having been second fastest on intermediates in Thursday’s FP2 session, Gasly again popped up in P2 after a superb lap in Q2.

Given that he took pole at Monza on a track also dominated by long straights, it looked like he might once again at least mix it with the cars from the established frontrunning teams.

However, in what was a messy Q3 session that saw several drivers tripping over each other ahead of the final runs, he wasn’t able to repeat that form, and he had to settle for P9.

“I think Q2 was pretty much the perfect track position, out lap, prep lap, and the way everything sort of worked out,” said the Frenchman. “Tried to do the same in Q3, and unfortunately, it didn't quite work out the same way.

“I think in that first run, there were definitely a few things we could have managed better as a team. But yeah, I think we'll review it all to get stronger and work well. In that second run, it was just a bit, I would say, unlucky, and all these cars bunched up. I had to slow down, open the gap, cold tyres into Turn 1.

“I lost two or three tenths into the first sector, and that was... I feel like at least a position was on the table. I lost by a hundredth to Lawson. So that was clearly at least one position more to get.”

Gasly was sure that he could have finished further up the order, even if a Monza repeat was never on the cards.

“It did feel like everything kind of worked out perfectly [in Q2],” he said. “And I was hoping we at least can get the same, which I think would have put us with Hadjar, like P6 or something like that, in front of Lindblad.

“But honestly, the tow was insane. If you look at the last sector of Antonelli, you just understand how powerful. I've never, ever felt that in a car. So yeah, there was a lot down to that. Unfortunately, it just didn't quite work as we wanted.”

Colapinto, meanwhile, was an encouraging P7 in Q2 but, like Gasly, he also had a difficult final session and thus had to settle for P10.

“I think we are all very disappointed,” he admitted. “The last run was a mess. The previous one, I just took a bit of a feel. I was fully alone on track. So in the straights, I was very slow. But I think the pace was generally there. We had a few places where we could do better and we could improve.

“But the session was just very up and down. Like, some laps you had grip, some laps not. And then the last lap, with all the mess in the prep lap, having a bit of a, of a race together, was not good. So the tyres were not in a good place.

“The lap was nowhere in terms of sector one: no grip, sliding everywhere, tyres very far out the window. And that here matters a lot when you don't do things correctly because of traffic or positioning. You pay it really hard later on in the lap.”

Like Gasly, he stressed the value of the tow: “You probably lose around eight/nine tenths in the straights only with no tows. It's big, and that means in the race it's going to be fun because it's going to create some moves.

“But in quali, it's tricky because you want to be behind someone, but you want also a good prep lap. And if people in front are not doing what you want, then it becomes a real mess.

“It was a pity because I think no run was maximised, so then you always have this feeling that could have been a lot better. But it is what it is – we have to focus on tomorrow.”
