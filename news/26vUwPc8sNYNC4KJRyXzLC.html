What time is the 2026 Singapore GP and how can I watch it?

Here are all the timings – along with all the additional information you need – for the 17th Grand Prix weekend of the 2026 season from Singapore.

Formula 1 moves onto Singapore for the final stop in a triple header, with the Marina Bay Street Circuit set to host Round 17 of the 2026 season – and here are the global start times for Sunday's race.

George Russell's title hopes suffered another blow as he retired from the Bahrain Grand Prix in Malaysia, enabling team mate Kimi Antonelli to extend his lead in the drivers' standings to a commanding 84 points. But even he couldn't match Max Verstappen, who clinched victory for the first time this year – will he be able to repeat the feat in Singapore, or will Antonelli return to the top?

Head to the RACE HUB or scroll down for more on how and when you can watch the action, along with the global start times for each session.

Singapore will hold the final Sprint of the season, which means drivers will only have one hour of Free Practice to find their rhythm at the track. That will begin at 1630 local time on Friday, 9 October before Sprint Qualifying follows at 2030.

The Sprint gets underway at 1700 on Saturday, 10 October ahead of Qualifying under the lights at 2100, while the Grand Prix itself takes place at 2000 on Sunday, 11 October.

You can enjoy coverage from every session live on F1 TV Pro (available in selected countries), which brings fans closer to the action via exclusive features like onboard cameras on all 22 of the drivers' cars, and access to both Pre-Race and Post-Race Shows.

F1 TV Pro can be streamed easily via Apple TV, Chromecast Generation 2 and above, Android TV, Google TV, Amazon Fire TV, and Roku. F1 TV Pro is free of ad breaks and available with commentary in six languages.

Fans can also watch via F1 TV Premium, a premier service that invites fans to step into the heart of the action with the ability to watch races in 4K Ultra HD/HDR on up to six different devices, as well as a personalised Multiview feature available on select devices with 26 different feeds to choose from.

Viewers can watch every F1 Grand Prix, F1 Sprint, Qualifying and practice session live in 4K Ultra HD/HDR at home, with F2, F3, F1 ACADEMY and Porsche Supercup sessions also available to watch live on the platform.

Alternatively, live timing will be available on F1.com and the F1 app across every session during the Grand Prix weekend.

Since making its debut in 2008, the Marina Bay Street Circuit has hosted every edition of the Singapore Grand Prix and delivered some of the most dramatic street races in recent memory.

The bulk of the action takes place at night against the stunning backdrop of the city skyline, but the temperatures and humidity barely go down despite the timing, making it one of the most physically demanding events of the season.

It's been the site of both heartbreak and triumph, with Sebastian Vettel winning a record five times between 2011 and 2019.

Conditions are currently expected to be very warm and humid, with temperatures reaching 32 Celsius. There's a chance of rain across Friday and Saturday which could impact driving conditions, but no precipitation is forecast for Sunday's Grand Prix.
