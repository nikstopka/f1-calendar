«Это совершенно другой уровень» — Алонсо о сложнейшем испытании «Дакара»

Fernando Alonso will compete in the 2027 edition of the Dakar Rally for Aston Martin ahead of the start of the next F1 season.

Fernando Alonso is always up for a challenge, and the Spaniard has made no secret of his desire to compete in other categories whenever he decides to finally walks away from Formula 1.

In January he will make use of a gap in his day job schedule by returning to the Dakar Rally raid event, which he previously sampled in 2020.

On that occasion he drove a Toyota, and this time he will be part of the new Aston Martin Dreadnought project, operated by category experts Prodrive.

He joins a three-car onslaught alongside nine-time FIA World Rally champion Sebastien Loeb and current FIA World Rally Raid champion Lucas Moraes.

When rumours of a Dakar return surfaced a few weeks ago Alonso suggested that it would be “impossible”, and he admits that a lack of time to prepare this time around will make his life difficult.

“I think I didn't change opinion,” he said when asked ahead of the Las Vegas Grand Prix weekend. “I think the Dakar in 2027 will be a much bigger challenge than it was for me in 2020, when I had six months of preparation. I went to different countries to test, included Qatar, Namibia, Morocco, Poland. It was quite an extensive programme. Plus, I did two rallies, I think, back then in Saudi.

“Now I had two days in Morocco, and then off we go. So that's a bit short, but at the end of the day, we decided together with the team, with Prodrive and with Aston, that it's still a good experience for all parties.

“Especially for me, because there is no secret that one day I want to win Dakar. It will not be possible this year, but I think it's an investment for the future, in terms of accumulating more kilometres and knowing more [about] the series.”

Alonso also stessed that the timing, with no clashing F1 commitments in January, made his entry possible.

“I think it makes sense,” he explained. “It's a month that we don't have much activity in F1.

"It's a fun car to drive. It's a nice experience on the professional side, and the personal side. I think it's one of the biggest challenges, as I said, because of the lack of preparation, of the ones that I attempted. But I’ll try to test in the first week, and to race hopefully in the second week.”

He also admitted that he’s up against specialists with vast experience of the cars and terrain, and that, as such, he still has much to learn.

“I think the people racing there, they've been doing that thing for ages,” he said. “They grew up in motorsport, inside rally, and with those types of cars, and reading different types of terrain and different shades, different lights.

“There are many things in Dakar that are very different. In F1 on a circuit it's just repeating the same thing lap after lap, and try to perfect that driving style. In rally raid it’s just improvising, constantly improvising, because you don't know the next metre what is going to happen – which has been one strength of myself, to try to adapt quickly to things and changes.

“But this is another level. I remember the first days when I tested in Dakar I had headaches in the night, and I think it's coming from the level of focus that I'm not used to. The eyes are... You cannot blink for four or five hours. While here, we change things on the steering wheel, we talk to the radio, we look at the screens, and we follow the race, because you are in the same little environment lap after lap.

“And Dakar is to the unknown; every metre is new. I think coming from my background, the challenge is a little bit bigger than if you grew up in that thing.”

While his 2020 participation gives him something of a head start, Alonso also feels that this represents a fresh start in many ways.

“I think I was competitive,” he reflected. “But I had, as I said, I think a good six months of preparation, and I had a good few thousands of kilometres before that, which I don't have now. And then my teammate now, Sebastian Loeb, is probably the greatest rally driver in history, and tried nine or 10 times Dakar, and he didn't achieve the win yet.

“So I'm aware of the difficulty, but that's why – because it's a very big challenge – that's why it's appealing, that's why it's interesting, and that's why I want to attempt in the future to try to win it one day.”

Alonso’s Aston Martin team is fully behind his Dakar programme, and the fact that there’s a branding crossover makes it even more relevant.

“Making the impossible possible,” says Chief Trackside Officer Mike Krack. “That's typical Fernando, isn't it? I think I said it earlier when there were rumours around, I think it's a good thing.

"I'm looking forward to seeing not only the ultimate performance, but the whole operation, Aston Martin being at the Dakar. That's a new thing.

“And then with Fernando, obviously, he has done already one Dakar, and we know that he was always keen to do it again. It is a challenge, I think, to do that at the beginning of January, and then the season coming straight after.

“But I'm sure he will manage his energy levels in the right way, and will be performing in the Dakar, and also when the F1 season restarts. We are all excited.”
