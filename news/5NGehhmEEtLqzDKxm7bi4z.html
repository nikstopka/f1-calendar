Вассер: «Последнее столкновение Хэмилтона и Леклера неприемлемо»

Ferrari Team Principal Fred Vasseur cut a very frustrated figure after another coming together between his two cars.

For Fred Vasseur and the Ferrari team, the Las Vegas Grand Prix brought a painful case of déjà vu as Charles Leclerc and Lewis Hamilton were involved in a costly tangle once more.

Just two races ago in Mexico, a first lap clash between the pair cost the Maranello team a shot at a potential victory.

That incident was followed by a summit meeting in Vasseur’s hotel room on the Thursday evening before the Sao Paulo Grand Prix, during which both drivers and their team boss aired their views.

The verdict after that gathering was that the air had been cleared and there was a new understanding between Hamilton and Leclerc. Indeed when they briefly ran together on track early in that weekend’s rain-affected race there was no issue.

In Las Vegas they both worked their way up the field after a poor qualifying session where both exited in Q2, and once again they found themselves fighting for the same piece of track for lap after lap.

Leclerc was heard asking for Hamilton to be “careful with the squeezing because it’s not going to end well,” while Lewis said, “Can you tell him to back the off because I’m trying to catch the guys ahead.” The team in turn urged Leclerc to be cautious with Hamilton.

However on Lap 37, everyone’s worst nightmare happened when they were battling for P6 and Leclerc knocked Hamilton into a spin, costing the latter four places.

Hamilton managed to recover to seventh by the flag, while a 10-second penalty saw Leclerc fall back to P10. It was obvious that the results could have been a lot better, but there was a much bigger picture for Vasseur.

“I don't know what could have been the outcome of the race, because it was quite difficult to imagine,” said the Frenchman afterwards. “But it's not the issue. The issue is once again it's unacceptable. It's a lack of respect for the team, for the sponsors. The same speech as after Mexico.

“I think we had a clear discussion in Sao Paulo. We passed the message to Charles during the race to think about the team when he was fighting. It's not the right word, but you can't freeze the position on this kind of race, because when you are in the slipstream, if you slow down, the guy who is behind you is overtaking you.

“And all the other teams they were able to overtake each other 20 times during the race – Antonelli/Russell, Piastri/Norris, even Isack overtook Max. Honestly, you can't imagine the frustration for me, but more important for all the guys at home.”

At the end of the race Vasseur took the unusual step of coming onto the radio and telling Leclerc that he wanted to speak to him before he attended any media activities. Asked what was discussed Vasseur made it clear that he’d voiced his opinion.

“No, it was not a discussion – I spoke!" he continued. "I think he apologised, he understands, blah blah blah. But the result is there, you do a mistake, you say my mistake, I'm sorry, but you did the mistake. Yeah, it's tough.”

He also explained that he spoke to Hamilton following the race in Nevada: “Lewis, what can he say in this kind of situation? I told him that we passed the message during the race to Charles, that he was aware.”

So is it time for Ferrari to impose different rules on the two drivers?

“Honestly, it's not a matter of rules, what you can do differently, except that you have to respect your team mate,” said Vasseur. “In this kind of race, you have to overtake the car who is in front of you. You can't stay behind. It's completely unmanageable.

“It means that you just have to do it with respect when it's your team mate, to take less risk, perhaps to come side-by-side and to come back behind.

“But you can't put it on the paper. And I think the race of today, nobody will write a scenario like this, and nobody can imagine a race like this. It means that they just have to act in a professional way.”

Asked why that wasn’t happening he added: “I think it's tension, probably the escalation of tension or whatever. It doesn't matter – it’s unacceptable.”

The obvious question from the outside is why the Sao Paulo message hadn’t got through.

“It's a good point,” said Vasseur. “It's why I want to once again have a discussion tomorrow, and also to Charles [who] will digest the situation.

"And I think it's not the right place to discuss with them two minutes after the race. I saw Charles a bit down. I told him what I have to say. I prefer to discuss with him tomorrow.”

Questions have been asked about Vasseur’s long-term relationships with both drivers, which goes back to their respective junior days, but he insisted that doesn’t compromise his role as their boss.

“It's not because I worked with them 20 years ago or 15 years ago that it's changing my position,” he said. “It's not an issue between Lewis/Fred, or Charles/Fred. It's an issue between a team and two drivers.

“They are employees of the team, and it's not that they have a good relationship with someone. And at least I have to forget it.”
