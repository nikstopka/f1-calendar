When was Qatar’s first Grand Prix?

The Lusail International Circuit had already garnered a reputation as a spectacle in MotoGP before it was added to the F1 calendar in 2021, making its debut as part of a Middle Eastern triple header to close the season.

As touched on, it was primarily designed for motorcycle racing, but has proven itself as an exhilarating venue for four-wheel action too.

The warm desert temperatures, high speeds and flowing layout create a unique challenge for both drivers and Pirelli’s tyres, with a 27-lap limit in place across every compound for the 2026 event.

What’s the circuit like to drive?

Drivers will have to move on from the cold weather they got used to last time out in Las Vegas and instead prepare for soaring temperatures in the cockpit, despite the on-track sessions taking place around and after sunset.

While there are minimal elevation changes, the high G-forces and relentless direction changes add to the conditions, making the circuit especially demanding.

Jolyon Palmer, former Renault F1 driver, explained: “Qatar is full of medium-speed corners and flowing sections.

“Given that it’s not a huge braking circuit, it’s just about finding the optimum downforce level for the car.

“There’s one good overtaking opportunity, and that comes down the lengthy start/finish straight.

“The kerbs can be quite punishing, so drivers will need to be careful there, but conversely, as it’s a night race, that will help the tyres a little bit compared to the afternoon heat.”

Where are the Straight Mode zones and overtake detection?

As a reminder, and as explained in our pre-season summary of regulation changes, Straight Mode is a different aerodynamic configuration that allows cars to reduce their drag, making them more efficient when accelerating up towards top speed.

The rear wing continues to open up a gap – just like it did when drivers used DRS (the Drag Reduction System) in the past – but now the front wing also moves. The uppermost front wing elements drop down at the same time the rear wing’s top element does.

This is a mode that is used on every single lap in dry conditions, in every area that is designated for it. Essentially, the car will adapt between two different configurations depending on where it is on the track, providing maximum downforce in corners but then less drag on straights.

In Qatar, there are four designated Straight Mode zones, commencing with a run along the start/finish straight, then between Turns 3 and 4, between Turns 10 and 12, and a final one from Turn 15 to 16.

Meanwhile, Overtake Mode replaces DRS and is a new power mode that allows a driver to recharge more electrical energy, and generate an additional electrical power profile, so they can sustain a higher speed for a longer period.

There is one detection point per lap for this mode – expected to be out of the final corner on many occasions, leading onto a long straight – and it will be available to drivers on the following lap as long as they stay within a second of the car in front at that detection point.

Around the Lusail International Circuit, the Overtake Detection point comes as drivers exit Turn 16, while the Overtake Activation line follows at the beginning of the start/finish straight.

Five fun facts about the Qatar Grand Prix

- Max Verstappen has won three of the four Qatar Grands Prix that have taken place (the race didn’t feature in 2022), with Lewis Hamilton the only other driver to have triumphed in the standard format - McLaren’s Oscar Piastri claimed his first victory of any kind in the Sprint at the Lusail International Circuit back in 2023 - Unlike Red Bull, Mercedes and McLaren, Ferrari have never led a lap in Qatar - The circuit was built in just over a year and hosted its inaugural MotoGP round in 2004 - Over 1km of the track’s total length is made up of the main straight, which provides plenty of overtaking opportunities into Turn 1
