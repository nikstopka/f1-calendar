Все, что вам нужно знать о трассе Лас-Вегас-Стрип

Get up to speed with all the key Las Vegas statistics, driving pointers, facts and more.

Formula 1 returns to action with the Las Vegas Grand Prix this weekend. But how much do you know about the Las Vegas Strip Circuit? F1.com presents your all-in-one guide…

Key Las Vegas statistics

- Circuit length: 6.201km - Number of turns: 17 - Number of laps: 50 - Race distance: 309.958km - Lap record: 1m 33.365s – Max Verstappen (2025)

When was the Las Vegas Strip Circuit’s first Grand Prix?

Las Vegas previously staged two F1 races in the early 1980s under the name of the Caesars Palace Grand Prix – but when the City of Lights returned to the calendar in 2023, it saw the debut of the brand-new Las Vegas Strip Circuit.

Located in the heart of the city, the 17-turn street circuit sweeps past iconic sights such as the Caesars Palace, the Bellagio and the Venetian.

Three years on from launching with a memorable maiden Grand Prix in 2023, the Las Vegas Strip Circuit will host its fourth Formula 1 event this year.

What’s the circuit like to drive?

With average speeds similar to Monza – the famed Italian track known as the ‘Temple of Speed’ – the Las Vegas Strip Circuit provides plenty of opportunities for action and overtaking.

Jolyon Palmer, former Renault F1 driver, says: “The track comprises long straights and slower corners.

“The long straights will necessitate slashing the downforce levels, so the cars will feel inherently skittish before you even factor in the cool temperatures. Building and maintaining tyre temperatures will also be tricky.

“Turn 6 into 7 is one of the toughest braking zones, accelerating through a long left-hander before hitting the brakes for 7 – it’ll be easy to lock the inside front there.

“Turn 12 is also crucial as it punctuates two long straights. Finding the balance between braking well and preparing the exit is tough, and mistakes here are punishing.

“Having good braking ability and traction will be the key and, in general, corner exits will be crucial onto the long straights.”

Where are the Straight Mode zones and overtake detection?

As a reminder, and as explained in our pre-season summary of regulation changes, Straight Mode is a different aerodynamic configuration that allows cars to reduce their drag, making them more efficient when accelerating up towards top speed.

The rear wing continues to open up a gap – just like it did when drivers used DRS (the Drag Reduction System) in the past – but now the front wing also moves. The uppermost front wing elements drop down at the same time the rear wing’s top element does.

This is a mode that is used on every single lap in dry conditions, in every area that is designated for it. Essentially, the car will adapt between two different configurations depending on where it is on the track, providing maximum downforce in corners but then less drag on straights.

At the Las Vegas Strip Circuit, there are three designated Straight Mode zones: the first is situated on the run between Turns 4 and 5, followed by the long section between Turns 12 and 14, and a final zone between Turns 16 and 17.

Meanwhile, Overtake Mode replaces DRS and is a new power mode that allows a driver to recharge more electrical energy, and generate an additional electrical power profile, so they can sustain a higher speed for a longer period.

There is one detection point per lap for this mode – expected to be out of the final corner on many occasions, leading onto a long straight – and it will be available to drivers on the following lap as long as they stay within a second of the car in front at that detection point.

The Overtake Detection marker here comes just out of Turn 16, while the Overtake Activation line follows on the straight towards Turn 17.

Five fun facts about the Las Vegas Grand Prix

- The Las Vegas Grand Prix is held on a Saturday, marking a change from the usual weekend format - Max Verstappen has the best record so far at the event, having won the inaugural race in 2023 before scoring another victory in 2025 as well as setting a new lap record - The race is the only one on the calendar where McLaren have raced more than three times and never finished in the top five, with both cars disqualified in 2025 due to technical infringements - A different driver has claimed pole position at every Las Vegas Grand Prix to date – Charles Leclerc was the first to do so in 2023, followed by George Russell and Lando Norris - Las Vegas became the third race on the calendar to be held in the United States when it arrived in 2023, joining Miami and Austin
