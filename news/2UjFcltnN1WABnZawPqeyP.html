Что сказали команды – День гонки в Лас-Вегасе

The drivers and teams report back on all the events from the 2026 Las Vegas Grand Prix.

Red Bull

Verstappen drove a masterclass in Las Vegas to record his third win here and fourth of the season. He led off the line, but was soon challenged by those behind having a tow. Despite losing the lead multiple times, every time the Dutchman kept calm and retook the place. Russell, Norris and Antonelli threw everything they could at the Red Bull man, but no one could make it stick.

Verstappen even had enough composure to debate deployment strategies with the team, worried about being passed last on the final lap and not having time to come back at his rival. But Antonelli’s late issue meant the last lap was the only one where Verstappen had some breathing room from the entire race.

As for Hadjar, his pace was very strong. He ran second and even challenged Verstappen at times. He looked on for a podium until he suffered an apparent front wing failure late on, the resultant pit stop dropping him out of the points. The Frenchman was also handed a 10-second post-race penalty for slowing down before the pit stops, as he attempted to create a gap with Red Bull double stacking their drivers.

“It was definitely one of the craziest Grands Prix I’ve ever experienced. The whole race was about managing the deployment, timing everything correctly and making sure you stayed ahead of the cars behind. They were constantly changing their strategy as well, so you always had to be aware of what was happening around you and make the right decisions. I just wanted to stay in the lead and not drop back into the pack. It was very unusual today and something completely different to what we normally experience, but the most important thing is that we won. We’ve been strong over the last few races, consistently scoring podiums, and the turnaround from the team has been very positive. We did everything right today and I’m happy we came away with the win.”

“A disappointing end to what was a really promising first half of the race. The start was great, and it was fun to be part of what was a crazy battle. We were fighting at the front and had a lot of pace. Unfortunately, the front wing took unexpected damage and we had to box, which took us out of the fight for at least a podium. We knew we’d be fast here, and we were. The car felt so strong through the corners and, with the natural tow, we were able to keep up on the straights. It’s a shame, as everything was looking great, but that’s racing sometimes.”

“What an incredible race and an outstanding performance from Max and the entire Team. We knew that energy management and the slipstream would play a significant role today, but I don't think anyone anticipated quite so much overtaking. Max was simply exceptional, both in attack and defence, keeping his composure right until the very last lap. The communication between Max, his engineers and the pit wall was outstanding, and that teamwork made a real difference in such an unpredictable race.

"At the same time, it is a real shame for Isack. He had fantastic pace, was right up there with Max and at one stage we were running P1 and P2. He deserved so much more from this weekend, and we owe him an apology for what happened today. we will analyse what happened with his front wing and come back stronger in Doha.

"We started the weekend on the back foot, but the way everyone responded, took risks and worked together to turn things around was remarkable. This victory is another fantastic reward for the hard work of everyone in the Team.

"We will enjoy the moment tonight, but then our focus will turn to the last two races of the season and trying to perform at that level again there.”

McLaren

Norris started from P3, but he struggled on the soft tyres and dropped all the way out of the top 10 in the first stint. Piastri was going better on the mediums and soon climbed back into the top 10 and was even running ahead of his team mate at times. Somehow despite multiple moves, those two kept their battles clean.

The Australian lost out in the double stack behind the Safety Car, but soon climbed back to where his team mate was running. On the mediums, they looked much more competitive and weaved their way forward, with Norris even picking off Russell and Verstappen and leading for a few corners at one point.

They were behind the two Mercedes cars when Russell ran off, and both picked off Antonelli right at the end to score what, at times this weekend, looked a very unlikely double podium.

“It was a chaotic race from start to finish, and every lap brought something to manage: battery use, positioning, slipstreams and whether to attack or defend. I was down the order early on, and it took time to understand how to use the battery and get the most from the car. Once we worked that out, we started making progress. The cornering speed was strong, but our lack of straight-line speed made it difficult to move forward and hold position. We timed the opportunities well, especially in the closing laps, and second place was the best we could achieve. I’m very happy for the team to come away with a double podium.

“We weren’t expecting this result, particularly at a circuit where we knew we might struggle. The team did a great job, and we’re making progress, but there is still plenty we want to improve. Qatar will be a very different challenge, so we’ll keep working, stay reasonably optimistic and see where we are when we get there.”

“I am very happy to be back on the podium, especially on a day where we weren't expecting it. It's a great result for the team and to have both Lando and I on the podium, making up 14 positions on my side, is exactly what we needed.

“We were able to maximise across a lot of different aspects to be able to put it all together. We were able to get through the pack well both times, and the overtaking was good fun. While we have work to do on it, our straight-line speed felt okay, especially with the power of the slipstream, and our overall pace was good. Our strength in the corners meant we were able to close up there, which was positive.

“For the final two races, we hope to be quick and to keep working at the points gap to Red Bull. Thank you to the team for their work so far. Today felt like a step in the right direction, and while it was a unique race on a very different circuit, let's see if we can carry this strong momentum into the final two races of the season.”

“What a race! Las Vegas treated all Formula 1 fans to an incredible spectacle: 50 laps of thrills and overtaking manoeuvres that will go down in the history of the sport and represent the best possible promotion for Formula 1 in the US.

“For us, there is also the double joy of seeing both our drivers on the podium and of being the team that scored the most points on a circuit where our car was clearly not competitive enough to achieve such a result. This is the result of the work done first and foremost by Lando and Oscar, who probably delivered one of the best races of their careers. The support they received from their respective engineering teams, assisted by the HPP staff, was also crucial: finding the best way to manage energy and slipstream today was truly complicated, especially in a race that offered not a moment’s respite from the start right through to the chequered flag.

“Lando managed to find the right solutions to fight his way back after an early stage in which he paid dearly for the MCL40’s lack of top speed on this circuit. Oscar staged a superb comeback from 17th on the starting grid, and the podium is a fitting reward for a performance of the very highest standard. I was particularly pleased to see how both of them battled it out at various stages of the race, whilst bearing in mind that the team’s interests are always the priority.

“With this result, we’ve managed to close the gap on Red Bull: nine points separate us with two races still to go. Now we must prepare as best we can for the final rounds, first in Qatar and then in Abu Dhabi, where we believe the MCL40 – thanks in part to the updates we introduced between São Paulo and Las Vegas – can be competitive and allow Lando and Oscar to fight for victory once again.”

Mercedes

A case of what might have been for Mercedes. Russell was the first on the attack, repeatedly passing Verstappen for the lead only to lose out a few corners later. Meanwhile, while he was battling for first, occasionally losing second and fighting with Hadjar for good measure, Antonelli was climbing back through the field. He was the only front runner to start on the mediums, which hampered him off the line.

On the softs late on Antonelli was able to pass the two McLaren cars and Gasly to run third, and then he picked off his team mate. He took his turn passing Verstappen for the lead, only to be re-passed a few corners later. But there was a late sting in the tail as Russell made a move on Antonelli on the penultimate lap, locked up and hit the barriers which dropped him from P3 to ninth. Then on the last lap, Antonelli played with his deployment to make sure he had battery to attack Verstappen through the last few corners.

Instead, that left him with no battery and he was picked off by both McLaren cars to drop off the podium, the Italian doing well to hold Gasly at bay and stay in fourth.

"That was a really unusual and exciting race, although obviously it is frustrating to finish the way we did. We were in the fight for the win throughout and, going into the final lap, I wanted to stay behind Max (Verstappen) and keep the overtake mode available to give myself the best possible opportunity on the final straights. I lifted to try to achieve that, but unfortunately it compromised the battery deployment, and I came out of the chicane with almost no energy. That meant I couldn’t attack him and we also lost the podium.

"It was disappointing because I genuinely felt we had a chance to win today. Before that, though, there were plenty of positives. There was so much back and forth racing, and you had to be very strategic with your positioning and energy management. I learned a lot from the race and also from these conditions, where I know I still have room to improve. We have two races left and we want to continue to fight for wins and finish the season strongly."

"That was an incredibly unusual race and obviously I’m disappointed with how mine ended. The slipstream was massive and the different energy management strategies meant the order was constantly changing. With a few laps to go, I knew that if I wanted to fight Max (Verstappen) for the win, I needed to be P2 going on to the final lap. I saw the opportunity to pass Kimi, went for it, but unfortunately got it slightly wrong, locked up and paid the price. I’m not happy with the outcome, but I don’t regret going for the move. I could have been conservative and probably come home in P3, but at this stage of the season I want to fight for victories. It didn’t work out today, but at least we went for it."

Toto Wolff, Head of Mercedes-Benz Motorsport

"That was a fittingly thrilling Grand Prix for Las Vegas; crazy place, crazy race! We were in the fight throughout but are naturally disappointed with our result. Even when you have won both championships, you are never completely satisfied in motor racing. You still want to win every race, and today we had the opportunity to achieve more than we ultimately did.

"For George, he was looking to make the pass into Turn 5, got onto the dirty part of the track under braking and couldn’t make the move stick. That ended his chance of battling for the win. Meanwhile Kimi was trying to manage his positioning behind Max (Verstappen) ahead of the final lap, but the lift and subsequent reapplication of the throttle cost him energy deployment and, ultimately, the chance to fight to the line and his place on the podium.

"Those are small margins, but against this level of competition they make the difference. With such low degradation, there wasn’t a significant performance offset between the cars, so it became a really interesting race about positioning, judgement and race craft. We can appreciate the spectacle while still being frustrated with our own race. We know we could have done a better job today and we will look at those moments, learn from them and aim to finish the final two races of the season more strongly than we did today."

Andrew Shovlin, Trackside Engineering Director

"It's obviously disappointing to be running P2 and P3 with three laps to go, feeling like we had a shot at the win, to ultimately cross the line P4 and P9. It was an unusual Grand Prix from a racing point of view. Getting yourself in front was relatively straightforward, but staying there was the difficult bit. The combined effect of the tow and overtake mode made it almost impossible for anyone to break away, so much of the afternoon was spent in the bunch, jockeying for position.

"Kimi did a good job of steadily moving forward while George was busy racing Verstappen. The Safety Car came at a point that took away much of the strategic variation, so from there it became a question of how cleverly you could manage and deploy your energy. We didn't have a great balance with either car, at different points lacking front and rear grip. That meant we were losing ground through the corners and having to spend more energy to recover it on the straights.

"Going into the final laps, we were looking forward to an exciting finish. Unfortunately, George dropped out of contention when he out-braked himself into T5 and tapped the wall. Kimi meanwhile lifted into T14 to stay behind and retain overtake mode. That affected his harvesting through the final corner sequence and left him with no energy to defend from the two McLarens.

"We didn't need a reminder that we have plenty of work to do if we want to defend both championships next year, but we got one today. We'll be working flat out to maximise the final two races, but more importantly to learn everything we can from them and make sure we put ourselves in the strongest possible position for 2027."

Alpine

The Alpine had very good pace in Las Vegas, which was not a surprise considering how well they went in Monza. Gasly fought his way forward and was running third after the Safety Car period, but was told to nurse his car which was overheating.

That was a shame for the Frenchman, but he still managed to finish a very strong P5 which is his best result of the season. Colapinto matched his best result with sixth, after a decent race where he kept out of trouble, pulled off a number of good moves and was fighting wheel to wheel with the McLaren and Ferrari cars on merit.

“That was an enjoyable race and finishing P5 and P6 as a team is a very good result. I honestly feel like we could have been on the podium but we had to turn the power down throughout the race to manage our engine temperatures so we need to review why that was and what we can do better next time. It was basically a slipstream game, so it meant following closely the cars ahead to stay in the fight. I know it was tough for the team to be on top of a lot of things and everyone did a great job – from strategy to the engineers and the pit crew for a good pit-stop – it was all very well managed. It was a busy one for me at the wheel – focusing on attacking the car ahead while keeping an eye on the car behind as well as doing all the switch changes, there really was a lot going on. We are very pleased with the result and battling with Red Bull, McLaren, Mercedes and Ferrari throughout the race was very fun and hopefully next season we can do more of that!”

“It was a race right up until the chequered flag and was a lot of fun, even more so when you have a result like that and score points for the team. Generally, I'm very happy with how the race went and with the final result, equalling my best-ever finish in Formula One. We have been competitive all weekend here, so to come away with 18 points is a great reward for our efforts. It's good to have two cars up there and fighting with the top four teams, which we don't do every weekend. Naturally, when you're in the mix, you also want a bit more, but that's a good feeling to have as you're never fully satisfied and shows we were competitive this weekend. It was fun behind the wheel and probably fun to watch also on TV and in the stands, but it was also quite strategic with using the tow, managing energy levels and temperatures. I'm happy for the whole team at Enstone for this result, which they deserve especially after the difficulties we've had in recent races.”

“It was a fantastic team result today, and both Pierre and Franco did a super job and drove well in a race that required a lot of precision and concentration. The racing was close and exciting which was great for Formula One. This result gives us a lot to be optimistic about and also gives the team something to celebrate at Enstone after a tough run of results in the second half of the season. Overall, it has been a very strong weekend for the whole team in Las Vegas and, with 18 points scored, it is also our best result so far this season. So well done to the whole team.”

Ferrari

Ferrari leave Las Vegas with another headache to sort. The race was going surprisingly well for them after their struggles in Qualifying, with Hamilton climbing back through the field and passing his team mate cleanly. But he was handed a 10-second time penalty for overtaking Norris off track. When everyone pitted under the Safety Car, he was left out – had he come in for tyres and to serve the penalty, the double stack would have hampered Leclerc.

Hamilton therefore dropped to P14 when he did pit next time around, and sounded suitably frustrated by the turn of events. But he soon recovered back to where his team mate was running. Hamilton again passed Leclerc, but late on the Monegasque tried to retake the place and the two made contact. Hamilton came off worse, spinning as he dropped to P10. He recovered a couple of places, while Leclerc finished sixth on the road – but he was blamed for the incident by the stewards and had a 10-second time penalty applied when he crossed the line.

And based on Leclerc’s radio post-race, the team were not too happy with the move from the Monegasque driver.

"The team did a great job with the strategy and we had strong race pace. I’m proud of everyone in the garage and my engineers for all their hard work.

"I felt a podium was on today but it was a tough race and the penalty, followed by the contact, made that difficult. I kept my head down and kept pushing and we fought our way back into the battle. There are positives to take from today, even if the result doesn’t fully reflect what I think it could have been."

"The race didn’t finish well for us, and my mistake was part of that. I accept the penalty and we will review what I could have done differently so it doesn’t happen again. I’m sorry for the team, for everyone in Maranello and for the points we lost today."

"For the second time in three races, something unacceptable has happened between our drivers, and this cannot continue. We sent a very clear message to both drivers in Brazil, and today during the race we told them to think about the team when racing each other. Of course, in this kind of race you cannot freeze the positions because, with the slipstream, if you slow down, the car behind will overtake you. But our drivers should have handled the situation differently, as other teammates did both in front of and behind them. Charles knows he made a mistake and apologised, but that doesn’t change the result.

"It’s difficult to put into words how frustrating today’s episode is for the whole team. The pace was good and the podium was not out of reach. Once again, what happened shows a lack of respect for the team, for everyone working at the track and back in Maranello, and for all our partners. We will discuss what happened internally in the coming days."

Williams

Williams expected to go okay here relatively speaking, and so it proved for Sainz. He made some moves in the first stint, but emerged from the pit stops in the top 10 thanks to a few rivals losing out having to double stack. Sainz did not have to do so given his team mate was not even in the race – overheating brakes on the grid meaning the unlucky Albon did not even take the start.

Sainz then ran in the top 10 for the rest of the race, losing a couple of positions but then inheriting P8 courtesy of Russell’s late mistake, and Leclerc’s time penalty. Eighth is Sainz’s best result of the year and the team’s first points since Baku.

“I’m very happy and proud of our performance today! P8 might not seem much, but we delivered on track and executed a flawless race. I did the best start of the season and we took every chance that was available to us, making sure to save my battery when it mattered, which helped me overtake George over the line for P8. It was consistent qualifying laps for the whole race, so I’m glad it paid off and we could bring home some points for the team after a long season."

“It was a frustrating weekend, and obviously disappointing not to get to race here in Las Vegas. We had a brake overheating issue on laps to grid that caused too much damage for us to race. We'll investigate further to fully understand what happened.

"Carlos had a great drive, and it's nice to see the team in the points today. Looking ahead, we'll regroup and head to Qatar focused on making the most of the weekend."

“What an exciting finish to this Grand Prix. It is always spectacular here and today if you could stay in the tow everything was possible. Well done to Carlos, he managed the last few laps perfectly and executed a really great manouvre to keep energy to cross the line into a brilliant P8. For Alex, unfortunately it was over before it started. He was warming the brakes on the laps to grid and the rears caught fire – when they did, they burned the safety tethers which stop the wheels coming away from the car in an accident. Once those are damaged it means a complete suspension change, and we simply couldn’t do that before the race started. It was a travesty, because I know Alex would have been competitive today. It was great to see Carlos able to fight for points on merit and that is what we're pushing for next year, using Qatar and Abu Dhabi to keep learning for the future."

Audi

Audi did not manage to finish in the points in Las Vegas, coming close yet again but with no joy. Nonetheless, both drivers fought hard and were in the thick of the action. However, they will not be too happy with having a car finish 11th for the 11th time this season.

They did have a fun fight with the Haas cars, Bortoleto managing to just about keep rival Bearman at bay. But the team are running out of time to catch Haas in the standings, remaining three points adrift with two rounds remaining.

“It was a bit of a mixed race today. We had some good fighting after the Safety Car, especially with Gabi and Ollie, which was fun. The rest of the race was fairly quiet for me. Once you lose the tow around here, you give away so much lap time on the straights, which makes it difficult to recover. I think that was pretty much the maximum we could do today.

“Energy management was a bit of a curveball and felt like a moving target at times, so there's certainly plenty for us to understand and take away from this race. It's all valuable experience for us as a team, and we'll have a good look at everything to see how we can improve.”

“It was an eventful race in terms of overtakes and how much was happening, but overall, it wasn’t the easiest for us. We have struggled with issues on my car throughout the weekend, and I am disappointed because at one point I was up to P8 and really thought I could stay in the points. Unfortunately, it was difficult to keep up with the cars around me, but I gave my best and I think we did everything we could today. Now, I want to move forward and focus on the last two races of the year. Hopefully, we can have a couple of clean weekends and bring home some points.”

“Formula One put on a fantastic show tonight. With plenty of overtaking and action throughout the field, the fans certainly got a taste of what Las Vegas is all about.

“On our side, we finished P11 and P12. We knew it was going to be a tough race, and it was certainly full of action for us as well. We showed good performance at different stages of the race, but unfortunately, with the way the positions changed after the Safety Car restart, we lost some ground and weren't able to get back into the leading group, which had pulled away together due to the very powerful tow you get on this circuit.

“Both Nico and Gabi had tremendous battles with Ollie Bearman and the Haas throughout the race, with plenty of intense moments and some very close racing. Ultimately, we came very close, but finished just short of the points. Now we move on to Qatar and Abu Dhabi, and the fight is very much still on.”

Haas

Bearman was always up against it from the back of the grid, and opted to start on the mediums. That left him on the faster soft tyres for the second stint, which enabled the Haas man to climb up the order. As for Camara, he was running well until a collision with Perez. The Cadillac tried to overtake down the inside, Camara turned in and the two collided.

The rookie was spun into the barriers but managed to continue, limping back to the pits but coming back out to finish the race. The stewards investigated that collision, and found the Cadillac man at fault.

“I had a good first lap and had a lot of fun fighting with the Audis. I had some good battles with Nico and Gabi in that second stint. It was definitely one of the more interesting races of the year, it went by very quickly as we were all fighting so hard. At one point, I was in front of both of them, but that didn’t last very long. Before the race, with the team, we said we should finish P14, and we finished P13, so that’s a win in my book, but unfortunately we’re not quick enough to score points on merit. We have to keep fighting them and slow them down a little, and I tried my best to do that.”

“Some parts of the race were quite fun. We had some overtakes and it’s always good to be battling, but towards the end, it was a bit more boring being alone, then I got overtaken by Hadjar and Lawson. I think overall it was a positive race and throughout I was improving. At the start, it was a bit hard to get temperature into the tyres and get a good first lap, but once we had everything under control, it was a decent race. There was a touch with Checo, but we need to review that. It’s very rare to get laps in before your first full season in Formula 1, and this weekend has been very useful.”

“Our drivers did a good job today. Ollie fought with the Audis until the very last lap, so that’s everything we had. With Rafa, we need to improve the starts, but it was a huge improvement from Brazil in terms of consistency and he’s learning so fast. Of course, here, the tow matters so much and with Rafa being in free air, his lap times don’t look that good, but in corners, his performance was very good. I’m really happy with both drivers. In Qatar, it’s going to be a very different challenge, but we’re going to give it our best shot.”

Racing Bulls

With two cars starting solidly in the top 10, this should have been a night to remember for Racing Bulls. Instead, it soon became a night to forget, with Lawson unable to take the start after a fuel leak was detected. He did eventually start, one lap down and thus was never in contention for the top 10. Lindblad though started very, very strongly and kept pace with the front runners. He looked on for a really big haul of points, but after pitting behind the Safety Car, the rookie lit up his medium tyres.

That sent him into a spin and ended his race, and to add insult to injury, the team’s biggest rivals Alpine managed to score with both cars. Racing Bulls still have a decent lead in the fight for P5 in the Teams’ Championship, but another result like this and suddenly that lead won’t look so comfortable.

"A very tough day today. We’ve had a really fast race car all weekend, so it’s disappointing that a fuel leak before the race meant we couldn’t make the start. The guys and girls did a fantastic job to fix the car and get everything back together, and even more impressively, get me out on track without any further issues. Starting a lap down meant we were never really able to get back into the fight, so it ended up being quite a lonely race. There are plenty of positives to take away from this weekend and carry into the final two races. We’ll keep pushing and look to come away with some more points."

"It’s really disappointing to end our race this way. Up until that moment, we were having an amazing battle with the top teams, which is something I hadn’t really experienced before in Formula 1. I’m still struggling to understand exactly what happened; I was warming up the tyres when the Safety Car was out and as soon as I got on the throttle, the car just spun. Ultimately, it was my fault and I sincerely apologise to the team, as it was a huge opportunity lost. We’ll analyse what happened and learn from it. For now, our focus shifts to the remaining races of this last triple-header of the season."

"First of all, we apologise to Liam for not giving him a car to start the race from the grid today. We had a fuel leak which was complicated to fix, but the mechanics did a great job to get it out when they did. Arvid unfortunately had a difficult race after losing control of the car under the Safety Car. We’ll need to look closely at the gear shift to understand whether that may have contributed, but there’s no doubt he had the pace today and was capable of racing with the top four.

"We’ll put this one behind us and head to Qatar in full attack mode. We’re confident the car will be competitive there, and we’re looking forward to putting together a strong weekend as we enter the final two races of the season."

Aston Martin

Aston Martin expected to struggle in Las Vegas, the long straight punishing their car. As such, neither driver was able to climb up the order despite the chaotic racing. Alonso wound up not finishing the race, although as he retired so late, he was classified as P19. Stroll at least did see the flag, the first time he has finished a race since Austin.

“We were lacking any straight-line performance tonight as we have all weekend. I was trying to follow the train of cars ahead, but with the lack of power, it wasn't possible to move forward and very difficult to defend. We were making up time in the corners but then losing more than two seconds a lap on the straights with this power unit. I then retired the car just before the end because it felt like we had a PU issue. We go to Qatar and Abu Dhabi next and will look to do our best for the final two rounds.”

“I was battling with Valtteri and Fernando and it felt like our own race at the back. We have no performance in the power unit, so we always knew Las Vegas would be tough for us and there’s not much to take away from this weekend. We made it to the finish so that’s a positive, but we still had the same issues we've had all year. We are missing around two seconds in our straight line speed, so Honda need to find more power."

“Our fight today was with Cadillac and we ended the race ahead of Bottas with Lance in P17. Given our pace and lack of straight line performance, that was the most we could achieve. Fernando was racing with Lance all afternoon, back and forth, but he stopped the car on the final lap because he felt there was an engine issue. Both cars ran the same strategy of Soft and Medium, pitting under the Safety Car. There was not much for us to fight for this weekend but hopefully we can be more competitive next week in Qatar.”

Cadillac

Cadillac were having some fun on home soil, but as Perez spied a gap and made a move when fighting Camara, it all went wrong. The gap closed as the Mexican tried to dive down the inside, and the two made contact. The collision saw Perez then hit the barriers and immediately retire, which brought out the Safety Car. The stewards declared Perez at fault, and handed him a five-place grid drop for the next event.

As for Bottas, he came home in P18 after pitting twice, unable to keep Camara behind in the closing stages.

“We had good pace and were fighting with the cars around us, which was positive. It was a good opportunity to try and make something happen and fight our way forward. Unfortunately, I then had an incident. I was alongside him (Camara) and expected him to leave me enough room to make the corner. I was planning to make the move afterwards, into Turn 16, but unfortunately he didn’t see me.

“By the time I tried to avoid him, it was already too late. He apologised afterwards and explained that he simply hadn’t seen me. It’s frustrating, because we were having a good race and fighting with other cars. We were making progress as a team, but unfortunately the incident made things a little more difficult. It is what it is, we move forward.”

“There was lots of action, which is nice! It’s good to be there fighting. Today the strategy was good, both pit stops were good and we finished the race, so lots of positives to take away. We’re at the stage now that we know we’re lacking pace but we know that next year can be very different. It was fun, on this track there are so many opportunities to overtake which makes a big difference. It’s good to finish the race, we got good mileage and hopefully we can keep this momentum for the last two races this season.”

“A contrasting night for the team. It was an action packed race and Valtteri brought it home after a long battle with the Aston Martins. During the Safety Car we elected to stop on consecutive laps to get back on our preferred soft tyre. The team also executed two great pitstops. After a tough few races for Valtteri I am pleased he took the chequered flag.

“For Checo it was an unfortunate incident with Camara, which is frustrating, both for Checo and the team, but it happens when you race hard with other drivers. Pirelli

Dario Marrafuschi, Pirelli Motorsport Director

“We witnessed a very interesting race, with plenty of overtaking and a high level of competition. The performance of the Medium and Soft compounds was in line with what we had already observed. Both proved very consistent over a race stint, with the C5 benefiting from a higher level of grip. Another expected factor was the reduction in graining compared to yesterday. The tyres remained clean and allowed the drivers to push consistently, making the most of their cars’ potential.

"The very low temperatures made tyre management more delicate, particularly during neutralisations and restarts. The drivers managed this aspect well and kept mistakes to a minimum. Lock-ups, while present, also had a smaller impact than might have been expected in such conditions. The neutralisation brought the pit stop window forward slightly and the teams took advantage of it, further confirming a one-stop race thanks to the low level of tyre degradation.”
