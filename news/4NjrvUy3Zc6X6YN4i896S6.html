Что сказали команды – пятница в Катаре

The drivers and teams report back on all the action from Friday practice at the Lusail International Circuit, ahead of the Qatar Grand Prix.

Mercedes

Antonelli topped the opening session for Mercedes by a decent margin, finishing half a second ahead of his team mate. His lap was set on the mediums, the Italian not able to extract the same pace from the softs. As for Russell, he ran wide like many others as the pack struggled for grip on the slightly dirty racing line.

Neither got a clean lap in on the softs in FP2 thanks to multiple red flags, setting their times on the mediums. But once again Antonelli was on top of the pile, with Russell closing the gap in second.

Kimi Antonelli - FP1: 1:23.217, P1; FP2: 1:23.077, P1

"It was a positive day overall and I felt good in the car from the first laps. We tried a lot of different things across the two sessions, and it was interesting to see how the car reacted to those changes. We gained a lot of understanding today and that should help us as we work through everything overnight and prepare for tomorrow.

"The car has been feeling strong, but there is still a lot of work to do. The front-left tyre is taking quite a lot of energy and that is going to be an important factor through the weekend. It also looks very close at the front. McLaren made a good step between the two sessions, and we know Red Bull are likely to be competitive as well. We need to keep improving overnight and make sure we are ready for qualifying tomorrow."

George Russell - FP1: 1:23.724, P2; FP2: 1:23.204, P2

"Overall, it was a solid Friday. FP1 was a little more difficult and I wasn't completely comfortable with the car, but we made some good changes heading into FP2 and things felt much better from the first lap of the evening session. The car was giving me confidence and that allowed us to have a productive session and put together some competitive laps.

"It's encouraging to see both cars near the top of the timesheets, but we know things can change quickly from Friday to Saturday. McLaren and Red Bull both look competitive and I expect it to be very close tomorrow. We've put ourselves in a decent position though and, if we can make another small step overnight, hopefully we can be in the fight at the front in qualifying."

Andrew Shovlin, Trackside Engineering Director

"It's been one of our better Fridays of the year. The car seemed to be working well out of the box and the lap time was coming quite easily for the drivers on the Medium tyre. We didn't get the most out of the Soft tyre in FP1, but that gave us some useful information heading into the second session. We didn't make any major changes between the sessions and, whilst George was a little happier and Kimi a little less comfortable with his car, the pace still looked strong for both drivers.

"Neither driver completed their Soft tyre laps in FP2 due to the red flags, so we decided to get on with the long run work. The race pace looked reasonable, and we gathered some useful information, particularly around the front-left tyre which is taking a lot of energy around this circuit. That seems to be a common theme up and down the pit lane and, with a bit of tuning overnight, hopefully we can improve the situation for the remainder of the weekend."

McLaren

Piastri got the better of Norris when both ran the hard tyres in FP1, before Norris returned the favour on the softs. McLaren were then one of very few teams who came out early enough to get clean laps in on the C3 rubber in FP2, both drivers able to set a flying lap before the first red flag. That lifted them up the order, Norris winding up third and Piastri fourth.

Their soft tyre laps were still slower than those of Mercedes on the mediums, meaning there is still work to do for McLaren overnight.

Lando Norris - FP1: 1:24.067, P4; FP2: 1:23.522, P3

“Good to be back on track here in Qatar. We’re in a relatively decent position, and our baseline pace looks comparable with Red Bull and Ferrari. There are still things we need to improve, though, and Mercedes look a lot stronger at the moment.

“We estimate we’re around seven tenths off the lead cars, so we know a single change is unlikely to close the whole gap. But we'll put our upgraded floor on tomorrow and see what we can gain. Even a small improvement will help, even if it won't entirely close the gap to the front.”

Oscar Piastri – FP1: 1:24.077, P5; FP2: 1:23.549, P4

“It’s been a decent first day here in Qatar. Our pace looked pretty reasonable overall, and the car feels decent. There are a few things we need to work on in the longer runs, but, generally, it been a good start.

“Mercedes seem to be a step ahead of everyone at the moment, so we’ll need to find a little more pace to be able to catch them. We’ll get our heads down overnight and see what we can do tomorrow.”

“It was a very productive day for gathering important information for the rest of the weekend, particularly regarding tyre behaviour. We’ve opted to prioritise the Medium over the Hard for the race because we don’t think there’s a marked difference in terms of degradation, with the C2 delivering better lap times. With the double mandatory pit stop, there will be less need to manage tyre wear, but we’ll have to see how the situation develops with regard to graining: it was a significant factor in the first session, but things had already improved by the second.

“In terms of performance, Mercedes clearly look the favourites: they’re very strong, especially in the high-speed corners of the final sector. As for the rest, I think that we, Red Bull and Ferrari are all very close, as has always been the case this season.

“We’ll have to try to find the right balance between performance on the fast sections and the slow corners, such as Turn 6. Tomorrow we’ll be fitting the underbody introduced in Las Vegas, which should give us a bit more downforce, but to be clear, it won’t be a game changer.”

Red Bull

Hadjar finished third in FP1, giving a good account of himself despite a mishap in the pits where he seemingly left his box fractions of a second before getting the go ahead. The stewards had a look and opted to hand out a fine. As for Verstappen, like his team mate he seemed to struggle with tyre degradation in the first session.

The second session was also eventful, this time Verstappen tangling with Hamilton when the duo were on different programmes late on. The stewards took no further action, as the Dutchman wound up fifth, just ahead of his team mate.

Max Verstappen - FP1: 1:24.133, P6; FP2: 1:23.576, P5

“That was a little bit of a difficult day for us. The overall balance of the car is not quite there yet, which is costing us lap time, so we are not happy. At the moment the Mercedes are pretty far ahead in the short and the long run - hopefully we can close that gap tomorrow. This track is unique and not an easy one to get all the things right, but of course we will try and work hard as always."

Isack Hadjar - FP1: 1:23.878, P3; FP2: 1:23.583, P6

“I think every team is fighting with a lot of balance limitations because of the track characteristics of this circuit. We've been working on finding the right level and continued that into FP2, where we found some improvement. There's still a gap to the leaders as the Mercedes has been extremely strong so far. At the moment, our goal is the second row, but we always make progress right up until Qualifying, so we can't be counted out yet. Definitely a strong first day and there's more to come.”

"Our main challenge today was finding the balance that both the drivers were happy with, which is key for this weekend. The understeer was much greater than we expected, particularly in FP1. Max struggled more than Isack in this regard, but we moved in the right direction with both cars for FP2 and made a step forward, although we faced some compromises as a result. There is still a lot of work to do. Mercedes look very strong in the high-speed corners, and it will be a big challenge to match them. We'll focus on ourselves first to ensure we get to where we want to be."

Ferrari

Leclerc beat Hamilton by a tenth in the first session of the weekend, the duo evenly matched once again. Hamilton did run wide and take out a marker board late on though, that misadventure at least not damaging the car. But the second session did not go to plan for the Scuderia. First, Leclerc spun off into the gravel and damaged his floor. As such, he missed a chunk of the session as the team swapped out that floor for a different one.

Then late on Hamilton parked up with a lack of power, climbing out of the car which had to be recovered. The team could have a late night identifying and rectifying that issue.

Charles Leclerc – FP1 : 1:24.473, P7; FP2: 1:24.099, P7

"Our competitors look very strong, and the gaps we are seeing at the moment are quite unusual. We need to understand where those differences are coming from and what we can do on our side to improve. We’ll analyse all the data tonight and give it everything in qualifying tomorrow."

Lewis Hamilton – FP1: 1:24.575, P8; FP2: 1:24.216, P8

"FP1 was reasonably straightforward and the car felt okay. We made a few small changes going into FP2 but the second session was more challenging and unfortunately we had an issue towards the end of the long run, which brought our session to an early end. We’ll go through everything tonight and see where we can make improvements for tomorrow."

Racing Bulls

Lindblad bounced back from his Las Vegas disappointment with a strong performance out of the box, finishing in the top 10 in the opening session. Lawson was a little further back after struggling with power issues in FP1. In the second session, Lawson’s PU looked back to full strength and he climbed back into the top 10, beating his team mate by a tenth. That result bodes well for tomorrow, when Racing Bulls will be targeting two Q3 slots.

Arvid Lindblad – FP1: 1:24.601, P9; FP2: 1:24.547, P10

"It was a solid first day here in Qatar. It's a very fast circuit and certainly presents different challenges compared to Las Vegas, but I really enjoyed myself and felt comfortable in the car. The team did a great job in providing a strong baseline setup, so overall I'm quite happy with how things went. That said, the rest of the weekend won't be easy. The race, in particular, is going to be challenging from a tyre management perspective, so we'll need to be very delicate and stay on top of that. I think we're in a good place and I'm looking forward to tomorrow."

Liam Lawson - FP1: 1:25.593, P14; FP2: 1:24.425, P9

"It's been a decent day for us today and we’ve got a fast car, which is positive. We made some big changes in the right direction between FP1 and FP2, which put the car in a good place. We’ll continue to review the data and make any final tweaks to bring it all together. There isn’t a huge difference between the compounds around here, which is likely to play a bigger role in the race than it will tomorrow. The rest of the midfield are looking strong, so we’ll have to see where everyone ends up in Qualifying. I’m looking forward to it."

"It's been a solid day today. There was a small issue with Liam's Power Unit in FP1, which wasn't responding quite as it should have. However, we cleared that up for FP2 when he was very much back on the pace. We also made some changes to our balance between the sessions, so both drivers ended up feeling much more comfortable. We have plenty of data from today, which we'll analyse over night to see where we can improve ahead of tomorrow. Our long run pace looks very encouraging, so if we can put everything together, we should have a strong race as we continue our fight for P5."

Alpine

Alpine believe they will be there or thereabouts here with regards the top 10, and that’s how it appeared in the opening session with Gasly in 10th and Colapinto 12th. But disaster struck for the Frenchman in the second session, Gasly getting on the kerbs and losing the rear. It was a high-speed spin, the Frenchman bouncing through the gravel before winding up beached. He missed out on some running as a result, while the team need to assess the damage to his car overnight. Colapinto had a little duel with Russell in FP2, but other than that kept his session clean.

Pierre Gasly – FP1: 1:24.874, P10; FP2: 1:24.764, P12

“Our session came to an early end after the high-speed spin in Free Practice 2. It is never a nice feeling to face the wrong way at those speeds, but it does not happen very often. We have a few things to understand as a team. With the red flag just before my first push lap, we were quite keen to get a run on low fuel under our belts straight away. We had a little more engine power for that lap and we had quite cold tyre temperatures beginning the lap. I entered Turn 14 quite fast, there was a bit more bottoming, and I ran wide onto the kerb and lost it with no chance to recover the car back on to the track. We have a bit of damage to the floor but I am sure we will get everything fixed and prepared for tomorrow. In terms of our running, it appears we are a little behind Racing Bulls again, so we go into the rest of the weekend chasing them. The car felt okay on my laps, a bit of understeer in places, so there are a few areas for us to work on and improve. Hopefully we'll be in the mix for Q3 and put ourselves in a competitive position.”

Franco Colapinto - FP1: 1:25.409, P12; FP2: 1:25.733, P15

“It has been quite an up and down day where we've really struggled with the balance and not yet been able to find a good rhythm around this track. There were a few issues and delays to the session which stopped me from doing some performance laps, so all in all it has not been the easiest of days. Tyre wear seems quite high amongst all teams, which is what we expected heading into the weekend. Even with the limit on the number of laps, wear and degradation will still play a factor here. The performance delta and feeling between the Medium and Soft tyre is something we need to look into, as a lot of teams seemed to extract more performance from the Medium. We are not where we want to be at this stage of the weekend, but there's still time to turn it around and hopefully be more competitive as we head into Qualifying.”

“It has not been our finest day and it is clear our car has a few limitations at this track, especially with its high-speed sequences. Both drivers have not been completely happy with car balance and the car is not quite where we want it to be at this stage. High speed tends to be a weakness of our car and, while we have improved over the season, it still brings difficulties for the drivers as we saw with Pierre at Turn 14, who went off in quite spectacular fashion. We will need to replace the floor as there is quite a big hole in it but, given the speed, it could have been a lot worse. There is some pace in the car and I think we are in a very tight midfield mix all competing for the final spots in Q3. There is a lot of work ahead of us tonight here at the track and back at Enstone on the simulator to see what we can improve with an aim of making the car easier in the high-speed flow for both drivers.”

Audi

Audi had a quiet opening hour of practice, with their drivers evenly matched but not quite fast enough to make the top 10. They split strategies, the only team to do so with Hulkenberg running the hards and Bortoleto the mediums. As such, the Brazilian was the only driver on the hards in FP2, as Hulkenberg swapped to the mediums. But in terms of the results, it was more of the same, both drivers just outside the top 10.

Nico Hulkenberg – FP1: 1:25.437, P13; FP2: 1:24.871, P13

“A fairly straightforward Friday for us, clean for the most part. Qatar is a very different circuit to what we've had recently, with plenty of high-speed corners, so there's quite a bit to take in and understand. We'll go through everything overnight, see where we can make some improvements and put together a good plan for tomorrow. The field looks as tight as ever, so the small details will make the difference."

Gabriel Bortoleto - FP1: 1:25.053, P11; FP2: 1:24.614, P11

“It was a positive Friday. We had a clean day and I felt I put together some good laps. We still need to find a little more pace to put ourselves in the mix, and everyone here and back at the factories is pushing hard to find it. Track position is especially important here, so we need a strong qualifying and a setup that helps us look after the tyres in the race."

Williams

Both Albon and Sainz had moments in FP1, running wide and kicking up plenty of dust. The team used the session to test out things for 2027, rather than chase lap time. They also had moments in FP2, neither keeping their laps entirely tidy as more and more gravel was kicked up onto the track. Despite the difficult conditions, they managed plenty of laps with Albon once again the pick of the duo on Friday.

Alex Albon - FP1: 1:25.692, P16; FP2: 1:25.276, P14

“It was a difficult Friday as the tyres are tricky, so I think it is maybe the same for everyone. The long run is the focus because the graining is difficult for all cars, and it won't go away. Hopefully we can come up with some solutions and ideas for tomorrow. The qualifying car and the race car need completely different things, so it's going to be a very hard compromise to achieve both, but we'll give it a go.”

Carlos Sainz – FP1: 1:25.992, P18; FP2: 1:25.839, P16

“We had a relatively straightforward Friday today. This track doesn't generally suit our car and it’s a tricky balance for everyone between pushing on this rougher surface and preserving the tyres. We were struggling with balance across the lap which caused some snaps in different corners, but with some fine tuning I think we can extract a bit more from the car. We’ll work on it overnight to get us in a good place for qualifying tomorrow.”

“I think this weekend is going to be dominated by graining; we saw in FP1 it was harsh on the front left tyre, through Turns 4 and 5 and then particularly into the sequence of 12, 13 and 14. We changed our approach a little bit into FP2, limited the low fuel running and then really tried to maximise the learning on the high fuel. The tyre compounds are the same for everybody; we just need to manage that as best we can. Coming to this circuit, we knew that it was going to be more challenging than last week with the medium-high speed corners here. We've got some work to do this evening and a few other setup changes that we've got in mind, and we'll put the best car out tomorrow that we can.”

Haas

Bearman said the team might struggle here, the track not suiting the car. Both drivers were down the order in FP1, but at least Camara got plenty of laps in and experienced more usual track conditions as he continues to learn what he can about the VF-26. It was much of the same for the team in the second session, Camara again shading his team mate as Haas experimented with their own ‘flip-flop’ rear wing. It remains to be seen whether they will use that for the rest of the weekend.

Oliver Bearman - FP1: 1:25.766, P17; FP2: 1:25.996, P18

“It was a tough day to be honest, we really struggled to find a good balance with the car on both low and high fuel. It felt like we made a bit of progress by the time FP2 came around, the lap that I was doing on softs before the red flag finally felt decent. On high fuel, we carried over some of the damage that I did to the tire in run one, with graining. I think the car is definitely better than it was in FP1, we’re making progress, so we’ll see what tomorrow holds.”

Rafael Camara - FP1: 1:25.685, P15; FP2: 1:25.962, P17

“We had two clean sessions and were able to run through the program and do everything we wanted. Overall, we learned a lot for tomorrow and it was very productive. It’s not an easy track, but every session I get more used to it all and you can take a bit more risk, especially with the wider run-off areas here. We need to keep working and try to improve the car, and on my side there are a lot of things to see as well – so we’ll keep working and be ready for what comes next.”

“It’s been a challenging Friday, as the car’s limitation is very different here from any other track. In FP1, I think we were too far away on car set-up, but both drivers managed what they could, and we improved the car for FP2 – Ollie, especially, was happier. Across the garage, we tried a few different things, and I think we could learn from each other. We still need to improve things, but I feel a lot better after FP2. We need to work on race pace, we have some work to do there, but it was trouble-free, and we got all the data, so time to get to work.”

Aston Martin

Crawford was driving again in FP1 in place of Alonso. The rookie gave a good account of himself, beating Stroll for starters. Alonso returned to the cockpit in FP2, but immediately ran wide and damaged his car, leading to a stay in the pits. When he did come back out, he managed to beat his team mate as Stroll also ran wide, kicking up gravel as he skating off the track.

Fernando Alonso - FP2: 1:26.046, P19 Lance Stroll - FP1: 1:26.416, P20; FP2: 1:26.648, P20 Jak Crawford - FP1: 1:26.306, P19

"All the races have their own unique challenges and this is a very different circuit to Vegas, with much warmer temperatures. You're pushing in high-speed corners here and always trying to be on the edge.

"There are some things to work on tonight to try to get the car in a better window for this track."

"It was a good practice session and probably my favourite one as well. The track here is pretty fun to drive in a Formula One car.

"It was a shame that Las Vegas FP1 didn't really happen for anyone due to the weather conditions, but I definitely feel like I've been learning about the car every time I've been out in it.

"There is a bit of work to do overnight to get the car in the best window."

Mike Krack, Chief Trackside Officer said:

"It was a tricky afternoon and evening of practice here in Qatar.

"It's a punishing high-speed corner circuit, with aggressive kerbs and gravel traps and we've seen a lot of cars going off track during both sessions.

"We've spent some time on repairs to both cars in between and during the sessions and we have some work to do overnight.

"It was good to see Jak get some more important mileage in the car during FP1 and Fernando then returned for FP2 and will continue for the remainder of the weekend."

Pirelli

"Qatar is once again proving to be the most demanding circuit of the season for the tyres. The race simulations carried out today, particularly during the second free practice session, confirmed expectations in terms of wear and degradation. Graining, encouraged by the dirty track surface and the level of aerodynamic load, was especially evident on the front-left tyre. This phenomenon accelerated tyre wear and led several tyres to reach very high levels of wear. It is a scenario that will make careful tyre management even more important for the teams ahead of Sunday’s race.

"Performance degradation also proved to be high, particularly on the front axle, to the extent that a two-stop strategy would probably have been the most competitive option even without the limitations introduced for this event to preserve tyre integrity. As for the compounds, the Soft proved competitive over a single lap, although some teams struggled to extract its full potential. The Hard was also noteworthy: despite being used by only a limited number of teams, it demonstrated remarkable consistency of performance. For this reason, the teams that have retained two sets of C1s could have a particularly interesting strategic option available for the race."
