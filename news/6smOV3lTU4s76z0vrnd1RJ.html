Что сказали команды – пятница в Мехико

Пилоты и команды отчитываются обо всех действиях с пятничной тренировки на Autodromo Hermanos Rodriguez перед Гран-при Мехико 2026 года.

Феррари

Ferrari была единственной командой, которая управляла обоими гонщиками на полный рабочий день в FP1, и оба провели достойную сессию, быстро смотря на жесткие и мягкие шины. Шарль Леклер с разумным отрывом опередил FP1, а Льюис Хэмилтон отыграл несколько мест после того, как широко коснулся своего круга. Леклер продолжил с того места, где он остановился во второй тренировке, снова ехал быстрее, несмотря на то, что заперся на мягких шинах и бежал широко на первом повороте с первой попытки на летном круге.

Но вторая сессия Гамильтона была не такой гладкой, так как он был вынужден припарковаться на обочине с проблемой ПУ.

Шарль Леклерк - FP1: 1:18.128, P1; FP2: 1:17.827, P1

«В целом наша производительность выглядит довольно хорошо, но нам все еще нужно работать над некоторыми из наших ограничивающих факторов. Сегодня на трассе было довольно сложно, так как поверхность была скользкой, поэтому мы будем работать над этим, чтобы найти некоторые улучшения на завтра».

Льюис Хэмилтон - FP1: 1:18.866, P4; FP2: 1:18.512, P11

«Это был не самый простой день для меня, так как я пропустил несколько заездов в FP1, а затем должен был рано остановиться во FP2. Несмотря на это, машина чувствовала себя быстрой, поэтому было жаль, что нам пришлось остановиться, прежде чем завершить длинный пробег. С точки зрения настройки, я не думаю, что нам нужно вносить слишком много изменений, так как машина работает хорошо. Мы изучим проблему сегодня вечером и сделаем все возможное, чтобы подготовиться к завтрашнему дню. Я готов максимально использовать то, что у нас есть».

Макларен

Leonardo Fornaroli had an eventful session for McLaren when he took over Norris’ cockpit. He locked up more than once, went for a spot of lawn mowing and sounded a little confused on the radio, but by and large he kept the car in one piece. As for Oscar Piastri, he had a quiet opener and finished inside the top 10. Roll on FP2, and Lando Norris looked strong, pushing Leclerc and winding up just a tenth off the Ferrari man. That was despite a big lock-up on the mediums for the World Champion, rendering that compound useless for the long runs. Piastri struggled by comparison, but he did catch one big slide that had the crowd on the edge of their seats.

«Неплохой день на табелях учета рабочего времени в конце Free Practice 2, но машина все еще бросает вызов вождению. Темп определенно есть, но на данный момент поле невероятно напряженное, поэтому все сводится к исполнению в завтрашней квалификации. Спасибо Лео за его поддержку Free Practice 1, у нас еще есть над чем поработать в команде, но у нас определенно есть фундамент для развития».

Oscar Piastri - FP1: 1:19.376, P8; FP2: 1:18.469, P10

«Мексика - это всегда вызов с высотой, низкой прижимной силой и очень небольшим сцеплением, но сегодня я чувствовал себя немного лучше, чем ожидал. Даже при неряшливом круге на Softs в Free Practice 2 базовый темп выглядит конкурентоспособным, и трасса быстро улучшалась по мере того, как мы шли. Мы многому научились в ходе сессий, и если я уберу области на коленях, которые мне нужны, я уверен, что завтра мы сможем улучшить порядок».

«Было очень интересно вернуться на трассу в FP1 с McLaren Mastercard, и я благодарен команде и Ландо за то, что они дали мне возможность пилотировать MCL40. Это был мой первый раз на этой трассе, и было нелегко сразу обрести уверенность, чтобы получить передние шины до нужной температуры на трассе, которая, особенно на старте, обеспечивала очень мало сцепления с дорогой. Фактически, блокировка в первом повороте испортила мой первый круг на Softs.

«Я очень рад быть частью этой команды и тесно сотрудничать с инженерами, всегда пытаясь предоставить четкую обратную связь, чтобы помочь развить автомобиль в полной мере. Для меня очень важно продолжать набираться опыта в Формуле 1, поскольку я развиваюсь как гонщик: я уже с нетерпением жду возвращения на трассу в следующую пятницу в Интерлагосе!»

"All in all, it was a very productive Friday. We devoted a significant portion of our early practice sessions to set-up experiments and evaluations, particularly to adapt the car to the specific challenges of high altitude. Leo did a fantastic job of supporting our test programme in the opening session, providing us with a solid foundation on which to fine-tune performance in the afternoon. Both Lando and Oscar completed their planned programmes, using all three tyre compounds and running with different fuel loads on a track which, as is traditional here, offers little grip on the first day.

"Based on what we’ve seen today, the level of competitiveness is exceptionally close, with the top four teams separated by very small gaps and the rest of the field closer together than usual. Managing power unit and brake temperatures will be crucial for the rest of the weekend, especially in race conditions when running in traffic. We’ve seen numerous front-wheel lockups across the field, but we know where we can gain further lap times and feel well prepared to tackle qualifying and Sunday’s race."

Mercedes,

Кими Антонелли широко бежал на одном из своих первых кругов на трассе, но после этого успокоился и выглядел относительно быстро на открытии, так как Фред Вести держал его в тройке лучших на завершенном показе. Джордж Рассел присоединился к схватке во второй тренировке и занял третье место на своем втором круге, показав, что он, похоже, готов к бою здесь. Это было на одну десятую быстрее, чем его товарищ по команде, а у Mercedes был очень солидный день открытия в Мехико.

Кими Антонелли - FP1: 1:18.787, P2; FP2: 1:18.044, P4

«Прежде всего, было хорошо вернуться в машину и чувствовать себя здоровым сегодня. Это была интересная пятница здесь, в Мексике; никогда не бывает легко получить информацию о машине, особенно в FP1, когда трек нуждается в тщательной очистке. Мы попробовали несколько разных вещей между сессиями, чтобы получить баланс машины в лучшем окне, и, похоже, мы улучшили вещи для FP2.

«Есть определенно больше вещей, которые мы можем улучшить, начиная с завтрашнего дня, и у нас есть над чем поработать в одночасье. Ferrari выглядела очень быстро, но мы сосредоточимся на себе, посмотрим, где мы сможем добиться большей производительности и, надеюсь, поставим себя в хорошее положение на завтра».

"Fred did a really good job in the car and set us up well for my hour of practice; it was good to get into a rhythm so quickly after sitting out FP1. Mexico is always a unique challenge because of the altitude and the low grip, and that feels even more pronounced this year. We've been running high levels of downforce at most circuits because of the way the cars operate on the straights, so coming here we don't have the same step up in downforce that we would normally have at this circuit. That makes the car particularly tricky to drive, and you also have the challenge of turbo lag at this altitude.

«Там невероятно тесно, и каждый бит времени круга будет иметь значение. Ferrari выглядит самой сильной на данный момент, что, возможно, не удивительно, учитывая характеристики этой трассы, но я думаю, что завтра это может произойти в любом случае в третьем квартале. Нам нужно максимизировать все, потому что маржа будет очень маленькой».

"My FP1 session today was really satisfying. We worked through our full programme, gained lots of valuable data both for this weekend and the longer term, and helped set up the rest of our day. Personally, I was also very happy with how I performed across the hour. I knew my soft tyre lap was a good one, but seeing P3 on the timing screens made me extremely happy. I've put a huge amount of work into this car with the team, going back to the start of its development two years ago, so to have the opportunity to drive it here in Mexico and put together a strong lap was very special.

«Я очень благодарен за доверие, которое команда оказывает мне, и за возможность показать, на что я способен. Эти сессии важны для всех нас, новичков, поэтому я рад использовать их по максимуму сегодня. Я чувствовал себя комфортно в машине, хорошо проехал круг и был очень рад финишировать на третьем месте. Это был отличный опыт, и мне очень понравилось».

Эндрю Шовлин, технический директор по путевому оборудованию

«Это был солидный первый день в Мексике. Кими и Фред оба хорошо поработали в FP1, особенно учитывая, насколько сложными могут быть условия здесь с высотой и низким сцеплением. Фред завершил программу, которую мы запланировали для него, и проделал отличную работу, чтобы занять третье место. Это дало нам полезную информацию для переноса в FP2, когда Джордж вернулся в машину.

"Mexico is the hardest track to prepare for as it's impossible to simulate all the effects of altitude. We use Friday to understand the various issues that we are facing and come up with an approach to managing those through qualifying and the race.

"From the power unit side, we seem to be in reasonable shape with good delivery of power out of the corners. The cooling for both brakes and power unit is always an issue here but we were able to do decent long runs on both cars and whilst we'd like a bit more margin, it seems like all teams are having to do some management.

"The field is tight and we wouldn't put ourselves at the front of the bunch from today's running. We're struggling with a lack of stability on entry that moves to apex understeer. The single lap issues are actually quite similar to the long run, which should make it a bit easier to progress, but it's clear we need to find a step if we want to be challenging for the front row."

Red Bull

Max Verstappen sat out the first session for Iwasa, who had a good hour for Red Bull. Isack Hadjar had a more difficult time of it, complaining about his steering wheel not being on straight, and also issues with the brakes. In the second session, Hadjar made his way up through the field with less to complain about. But his team mate did not have a smooth time of it, instead making contact with Lawson as the two jostled for position. That left the Dutchman with a heavily damaged floor, meaning he set his fastest lap time in a car that was definitely lacking in some downforce.

“Today was a tough session and we probably could have done a little bit better, but we weren’t too far off. It is a different kind of challenge here as the high altitude is always quite extreme. It is tricky with the grip and the sliding, but I think everyone else will be saying the same thing as it never feels great at this circuit. There is some fine tuning to do and we just need to find more pace in the car to make a real jump. We got a little bit of damage from the incident with Liam too. The Ferraris are looking quick in this session; this is a track where you need to have more low-speed performance so it should play into their strengths.”

Isack Hadjar - FP1: 1:19.545, P11; FP2: 1:18.237, P6

“FP1 was a tricky session for us with only a few push laps on the board, but we managed to sort the issue out quickly and get some long running at the end. We made some good set-up changes for FP2, which showed. I felt a lot more comfortable in the car and we were more competitive. There are a few things we need to tidy up, but the long run has seemed good so far. The day started a little rough for us but ended with some positives. I think we can be in the fight.”

“It was great to be back out there. Going into an FP1 session, you don’t really care about the ranking or the lap time; you just need to focus on doing the job. It’s been a little while since I last drove the car at Monza, but I could really feel the differences since then. The car performed well in both low-speed and high-speed corners, which gave me the confidence to push to the limit. It also felt quick, which always makes it more fun to drive. We managed to get some clear feedback that we’ll take into the rest of the weekend, so it was a solid session for me.”

“Ayumu did a very good job in FP1, where we got plenty of testing done. On top of that, he showed impressive pace for a young driver. In FP2, the performance of Max’s car was clearly affected by some damage, which we will be able to fix for FP3, whereas Isack’s session was solid overall. Still, we have a lot of work to do. The long run was more or less there, but we have performance to find, as the drivers are not yet happy with the balance. We are confident we can improve that with the set-up for tomorrow."

Racing Bulls

Nikola Tsolov was making his FP1 debut for Racing Bulls, and as a result his times were some of the most eye-catching. He wound up just two places and one tenth behind his team mate, Lindblad just scraping into the top 10 ahead of him. Arvid Lindblad had another good session in the afternoon, again finishing in the top 10. Liam Lawson’s FP2 will be remembered for some wheel-to-wheel action with Verstappen though, the two former team mates both winding up with car damage after fighting over the same piece of tarmac.

Arvid Lindblad - FP1: 1:19.478, P10; FP2: 1:18.389, P7 Liam Lawson - FP2: 1:18.455, P9 Nikola Tsolov – FP1: 1:19.604, P12

“Today was pretty decent overall. The altitude makes this circuit quite unique so FP1 was a little challenging, particularly as these are new conditions for the PU. The team made some good changes heading into FP2, and the car felt more alive. There are still a few things to work on ahead of tomorrow, but I think we’re in a good place. I really enjoy driving this track and I’m looking forward to the rest of the weekend. We’ll see what we can achieve in Qualifying tomorrow, but the target is definitely Q3.”

“It was a super special day today and something I’ve dreamt of since I started racing. The atmosphere in F1 is very different, it’s crazy. I came into the session without putting too much pressure on myself and the team really helped me with that in making sure I felt the best I could, so I’m really grateful for that. The car is certainly different to what I’ve ever driven, it was tough to get used to but I got up to pace pretty quickly. I ticked all the boxes for what we needed to get out of the session and I’m looking forward to improving at every chance I get. I really enjoyed myself and I’m looking forward to spending the rest of the weekend with the team and learning as much as I can.”

“A strong day for us. The car has worked well across both sessions. Nikola did a great job for his first time in this car and at this track; the pace was there and he did everything we asked of him. We did a bit of aero testing with him and one of his objectives was to help with feedback and pick up differences in a couple of the specifications we ran, and he did that very well.

The car was in a decent window in FP2, the pace looks good on both long and short runs. Liam had an unfortunate and unnecessary incident with Max , which I think they both could have handled better. When you’re going for an out lap, it’s really important you do the last sector fast, which they were both going for.”

Alpine

Pierre Gasly had a good opening session with a top six finish for Alpine, albeit with plenty of big name drivers sitting out. He was joined by Gabriele Mini, who was another making his FP1 debut. The Italian did well, although he did run over the grass at one point. In the second session, Gasly showed his FP1 result was no fluke with another strong lap to land solidly in the top 10. Franco Colapinto struggled to get up to speed after missing that first session, so he will be playing catch up tomorrow.

Pierre Gasly - FP1: 1:19.210, P6; FP2: 1:18.407, P8

“It has been a positively surprising Friday for us where we have had one of our better Practice days of the season so far. We arrived here expecting some challenges given the conditions and some uncertainties as to how the car would behave at this circuit. We have had two good sessions today where I have felt good right from the first laps of the day. The altitude here always makes the car feel strange but, compared to recent years at this track, I am feeling much more at ease. We have a good baseline to work from going into the rest of the weekend where we have a few areas to fine tune. It's not massive things which we need to improve, a bit on braking as well as getting the car more comfortable with some bumps in the high-speed, but it's quite small details. The gaps are also small, only a few hundredths between a few cars, and it will be about putting it all together when it counts.”

“Even though it might not look that way from the lap times, the overall feeling after today was positive. We were catching up a little after missing Free Practice 1, but we are not as far off as maybe the timings show. I made a small error in the final sector and did not get the most out of my Soft tyre run. I know exactly where I need to improve for tomorrow and will also work with the team overnight to put ourselves in a stronger position. The long run pace was also positive, so if we can qualify well, we should hopefully be in contention for some points this weekend.”

“That was an amazing experience for me to drive for the first time in an official Formula One session. Firstly, a big thank you to Flavio, Steve and the team for the opportunity, it was a truly memorable moment for me. As I left the garage, I was waiting in the pit lane with Lewis [Hamilton] in front of me, which was very cool. There were some legends of the sport out on track, people I have looked up to since I was a kid, so to be there with them was incredibly special. I would say the session itself was positive on our side. The team had a clear plan for me which meant some aero testing and data collection which will hopefully benefit the team in the future. I tried to stay consistent and build up the pace lap after lap. I did some good laps but if I am being critical of myself, I did not click everything together so that is something for me to look at and learn from. It's a very unique circuit with the conditions; tough on aero, tough on the power unit and there is a lot to understand and manage. That is all part of the learning process, though, and it was great to experience it all for the first time.”

“Taking the day as a whole, I think we can be largely positive and confident heading into the rest of the weekend. We won't rest on our laurels though and we know we still have work to do and it's incredibly tight, particularly on one lap pace. Pierre really got into a good groove today and spoke very highly of his feeling inside the car, which is something we want to carry over into Qualifying tomorrow. Franco also fed back that he has a good feeling and knows where he can improve. The high fuel running in particular looked strong, but we know the field is more closely matched in Qualifying, so we need to look at extracting everything over a single lap. Also, it was a great job by Gabriele today, which was his first time in this car, and he did exactly what we expected of him.”

Haas

Ollie Bearman had an eventful end to his first session, pulling into the pits with a smoking car that initially looked to be caused by a brake fire. He was swift to jump out, but it turned out to be a PU issue and Haas needed to change that unit between sessions. Esteban Ocon had sat out the opening session, but he returned in FP2 and immediately complained about the power steering. He was not happy later on either, when he was told to switch to the mediums for his long run, not the softs. As for Bearman, his second session was also disrupted, as he briefly parked in a run-off area before getting going again. He wound up needing a software change to his car.

“It’s always challenging when you miss out on a session, but Ryō did a good job and collected some valuable data. In FP2, we had a small issue at the start of the session, which we quickly fixed, and then it was about getting up to speed as quickly as we could. I still don’t feel super comfortable in the car, so there will be some adjustments to make and some work to be done overnight ahead of FP3 and Quali tomorrow.”

Oliver Bearman - FP1: 1:19.412, P9; FP2: 1:18.849, P14

“Overall, it’s been a positive day on track. We encountered a lot of issues in FP1, as we lost our latest spec engine, so we’re having to revert to the previous one which is a shame, as obviously there’s some performance involved in there. The car was a bit more tricky in FP2, so we need to understand why, but in general I’ve been happy with the day. I’m looking forward to tomorrow, I think things will be feeling better and I’m excited to see how it goes.”

“First of all, a big thanks to the team. I’m really happy to feel the step in terms of performance since I drove in Budapest, so hats off to the team. We completed the run program, had no issues on the car, and we’ve made a step forward for FP2 with Esteban being back in the car. I’m really happy I had a good session, so hopefully we can score good points on Sunday.”

“We made a very good start. The car was competitive straight away, and both Ollie and Ryō looked very comfortable. It was good to hear from Ryō, as the last time he gave feedback was before our update, so he confirmed what our drivers have been saying about consistency, predictability, and stability of the package. Ollie was very happy, in low-speed he was mega, even in high-speed – which is one of our weaknesses – he was doing a very good job. Unfortunately, we had a PU issue, so Ollie’s FP1 was cut short at the end. The team did an unbelievable job between sessions to replace the engine and other damaged parts to send the car out on time. In FP2, Ollie was less happy with the car, operationally it was okay, but less of a clean session, if you like. Fundamentally, the car is still performing reasonably competitively, so we just need to clean up some areas overnight and prepare for FP3.”

Aston Martin

Jak Crawford did not have the best FP1, after he was told to park up trackside with an issue. That ended his running, Aston Martin later saying a suspected ERS problem had curtailed his session. Fernando Alonso though had a strong opener, finishing solidly in the top 10. The Spaniard impressed again in FP2, winding up 12th and showing the car does have some potential around this track.

Fernando Alonso - FP1: 1:19.357, P7; FP2: 1:18.596, P12 Lance Stroll - FP2: 1:19.631, P20 Jak Crawford - FP1: 1:20.803, P19

"It has been a solid Friday in terms of performance here in Mexico, and we feel a little closer today than we have at previous events.

"It is always a challenging circuit, so it is encouraging to start the weekend on a positive note.

"The altitude brings its own difficulties, but we will keep working overnight and aim to get the most out of the car tomorrow and on Sunday."

"It was a decent day for me despite an early end to the session. We had to stop the car on track due to a battery issue, but still managed to get some good running in today.

"Everything was going to plan up until that point so still useful mileage in a Formula One car for me and I enjoyed it.

"It was my first time in the car with the new engine so lots of things to learn compared to my last practice sessions and also to take away for my work in the sim.

"It felt quite low grip out there today and obviously we’re also trying to adapt to the conditions as much as possible to get the car in a good window."

"Fernando looked quite competitive in both the sessions today. The car was working well in the low-speed corners, which maybe made us look a bit more competitive than usual.

"It was good to give Jak another opportunity in the car in FP1, but the battery issue cut short his session slightly. We need to investigate the issues with Lance's car because it was very difficult to drive.

"We will try and find solutions for tomorrow. As we see every year, cooling is a big challenge for all teams, and we need to keep an eye on temperatures across the weekend."

Audi

Nico Hulkenberg was one of the first to lock-up in FP1, locking both front tyres for good measure in what were tricky track conditions. But while that was less than ideal, what was even worse was the Audi team leaving a cooling fan on Freddie Slater’s car, and letting him complete a lap with it in situ. That led to a lengthy stay for the youngster in the pits as the team assessed the damage, and cost him some track time.

Gabriel Bortoleto regained his cockpit for FP2, but the team had found an issue with his car and he missed three quarters of the session as a result, only coming out very late on and thus goes into Saturday very much on the back foot.

Nico Hulkenberg - FP1: 1:19.608, P13; FP2: 1:19.023, P15

"It wasn't the most straightforward Friday for us. A few things didn't quite work as expected, and there are still some open questions we need to get to the bottom of. We've got a bit of homework to do overnight, but hopefully we can find some answers and be in better shape tomorrow."

“Not the easiest day for us. I sat FP1 out, as Freddie was in the car, but then we had a few issues before FP2 which meant I could not get much running in, completing only two laps right at the end of the session. Still, a big thank you to my mechanics for all the work they put in to get me back out on track for those final laps. It is a shame not to have had more time in the car today, but we will do our best to make up for it tomorrow in the last final practice session before Qualifying.”

“It was such a special day for me to finally get my first taste of the R26, and I am very grateful to the team for giving me this opportunity. It is something I have been dreaming about since I was four or five years old; overall, it was an incredible experience and one I will take a lot from.

“We stuck to our plan and ticked the boxes we needed to, which is part of the job when stepping in for FP1. Even though our running was slightly limited, I also took some good learnings from the session and got my first feel of the 2026 cars, which was pretty amazing. I cannot wait for Brazil now.”

Williams

Luke Browning and Carlos Sainz kept their sessions clean in FP1, although the latter was frustrated by traffic at one point. It was much of the same in FP2, with the Spaniard and Alex Albon managing plenty of laps but not troubling the midfield runners ahead. Work to do for Williams overnight, analysing that data if they want to make it out of Q1 tomorrow.

"It was a better afternoon out there for me in FP2, with the car feeling more predictable during the session. Pirelli

“Track conditions proved better than expected. Although grip levels were fairly low during the opening stages of FP1, the circuit evolved significantly from the outset, helped by the running of the support categories. As a result, the track offered better conditions than last year, as reflected by the particularly competitive lap times. In addition, the effects of aerodynamic load loss caused by the altitude appear to be less pronounced with the current generation of cars, which may help explain why lap times are so close to last season’s reference.

"During the first hour of running, most teams immediately chose the hardest compound both to familiarise themselves with the circuit and to help clean the track. Given that two sets of C3s could be considered surplus to requirements for the remainder of the weekend, this was also the natural choice for the cars entrusted to the young drivers. Both the Medium and Soft compounds performed very well, with a performance gap of around eight tenths of a second. On a circuit that naturally offers limited grip, the C5 was particularly effective in extracting its full potential.

"Data gathered from the long runs indicate a significant level of degradation for both of the softer compounds. This opens up several possible strategic scenarios for the race, ranging from a one-stop strategy, with more careful tyre management, to a two-stop approach. On the front-left tyre, we observed localised graining, which already showed signs of improvement between the two sessions. If track evolution continues at the same rate seen today, we expect this phenomenon to reduce further over the course of the weekend.”
