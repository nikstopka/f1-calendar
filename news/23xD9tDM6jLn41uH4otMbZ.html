Что может потрясти рынки ставок в Лас-Вегасе?

After the lights go out in Las Vegas, clued-up bettors will be poised to make smart betting decisions in real-time markets.

The Las Vegas Grand Prix weekend is approaching and Max Verstappen is the 7/5 (2.4, +140) favourite to win the fourth edition of this iconic race.

The Dutchman, who is aiming to win for the third time at The Strip Circuit in Nevada, tops the betting markets, closely followed by newly-crowned Kimi Antonelli at 11/4 (3.75, +275), Lando Norris at 5/1 (6.0, +500) and George Russell at 6/1 (7.0, +600).

These odds are likely to shift significantly after Qualifying, but the betting opportunities are far from over once the lights go out on Sunday morning with plenty of action expected within the in-race markets.

Our expert betting writers have looked ahead to the Las Vegas Grand Prix to pick out some important factors that could affect the race and lead to opportunities for bettors to benefit within those markets.

Pay attention to power units and energy management

The 1.9km flat-out section down The Strip provides the Las Vegas Grand Prix’s most dazzling spectacle, and it's also one of the circuit's best overtaking zones.

However, this straight - one of the longest on the F1 calendar - will not be well-suited to every driver, and we can get a good idea of who might struggle to hold position or move through the ranks.

Ferrari sacrificed their turbocharger size for improved race starts this year and that has seen them struggle to make headway on power-focused circuits, while McLaren’s shorter gear ratios have resulted in them losing speed at the end of longer straights.

However, the Red Bull Ford DM01 has been deemed the benchmark power unit by the FIA, while the Mercedes-AMG F1 M17 has shown tremendous performance throughout the season.

This means the likes of Mercedes, Red Bull, Racing Bulls, Williams, and Alpine could enjoy successful weekends.

First-corner chaos can shake up the grid

Russell managed to hold onto the lead from the start in the 2024 edition of this race, but 2023 and 2025 saw plenty of action off the line and this is likely to be the case again this year, especially with how difficult race starts have proved in 2026.

We have seen a host of drivers struggle off the line, and last year’s Las Vegas Grand Prix resulted in Norris running wide into Turn 1 and allowing Verstappen to take the lead.

Verstappen was also involved in the action in 2023, forcing Charles Leclerc off the track at Turn 1 and receiving a five-second penalty.

Both of these races saw either the Virtual Safety Car or Safety Car called into action, so bettors should also pay attention to any drivers who make an early dive for the pits under Safety Car conditions.

Don’t bank on undercuts

The undercut, which involves a trailing driver pitting early to leapfrog the driver in front, has proven an effective strategy across a wide range of circuits this season, but bettors should not rely on this tactic when making in-race bets in Las Vegas.

Cool night temperatures in Nevada can drop track temperature to single digits, and when combined with the slow speed of the pit exit, can cause the fresh rubber to lose heat rapidly.

This reduces the potential for a successful undercut, even with teams bringing Pirelli’s softest range of tyres to the Grand Prix.

Instead, teams may decide to extend their stint in the hope of seeing a Safety Car, so bettors may want to take a risk on drivers that stay out longer than expected.
