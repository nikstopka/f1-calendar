Сайнс хвалит «потрясающий» Гран-при Лас-Вегаса после «невероятного» восьмого места

Carlos Sainz and Alex Albon react to their very contrasting Las Vegas Grand Prix experiences.

Carlos Sainz enjoyed an "amazing" drive in Las Vegas as the Spaniard managed to bring his Williams home in eighth place, scoring for the first time since Baku.

Having qualified 14th, Sainz started down in P20 after a 10-place grid drop for taking a new turbocharger. His quest to make up places off the line was made slightly easier by two cars starting ahead not taking to the grid – his team mate being one of them, as Alex Albon was unable to start the race due to overheating brakes.

But Sainz still had a lot of work to do from the rear of the field, and he was proud of what he achieved throughout the 50-lap Grand Prix.

“Just amazing, honestly didn’t expect it yesterday when starting almost last, to get P8 in a year when we have struggling so much," he said. "I said this weekend was maybe our only opportunity to score points in the second half of the year, and the opportunity presented itself.

“I did an amazing start, incredible Lap 1 and then a great race. I think 50 qualifying laps to stay around cars that were obviously quicker than us, being strategic with deployment, being strategic with my overtaking and my slipstreaming.

“I managed to stay in the fight and managed to score four points for the team that I am sure is going to motivate everyone.”

Sainz climbed up the order in the opening stint before benefitting from a pit stop behind the Safety Car, which was called following a clash between Sergio Perez and Rafael Camara.

With many teams having to double stack their drivers Sainz, as the only Williams on the track, was able to get in for fresh rubber and get back out swiftly, climbing into the top 10 as a result.

From there he did not panic as some faster cars recovered back past the Williams, instead using his deployment wisely in a drive reminiscent of his win for Ferrari in Singapore in 2023, where he deliberately dropped back to give Lando Norris behind DRS, to keep other faster cars at bay.

“I think the team knows we shouldn’t get any points in the rest of the year, that’s why this was our only and last chance, given the relative competitiveness in other tracks,” Sainz said.

“I think I am just proud the team nailed a very good weekend together with me, we both did an incredible team effort to – the only chance that we had this year to get points, we managed to take it, which continues a bit the trend from last year, whenever we had podium opportunities, we took them.

“This year, whenever we have had point opportunities, we have taken them and executed very good weekends.”

But while there was joy on one side of the garage, there was despair on the other. Albon had been due to start 14th in Las Vegas, six places higher than his team mate, but his car was wheeled off the grid with overheating brakes, an unusual issue in the cool Vegas conditions.

Sadly, the team could not rectify the issue before the race began, leaving Albon with his second DNS of the season.

“You need a lot of blanking to keep the stuff that’s cold warm, we don’t really understand it at the moment, we’ll have a look,” he said afterwards. “[It’s] frustrating, that’s pretty much the year so far. We’ll see what we can do in the next few races.”
