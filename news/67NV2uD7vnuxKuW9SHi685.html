Изменения в RB22, которые помогли Ферстаппену одержать победу в Лас-Вегасе

Red Bull made tweaks to their RB22 for the Las Vegas Grand Prix, with a new front wing endplate to complement recent tweaks to the sidepod.

Max Verstappen took this car to victory in the race, but only after a difficult series of practice sessions where the team worked hard to give him the balance he required.

Part of the experimentation with the set up involved doing back-to-back tests between the front wing endplates.

The endplates were last changed as part of the successful Baku upgrade, but for Vegas the team brought an alternative with the diveplane lowered and shortened to look very similar to that used already on the Racing Bulls (see the illustrations below).

The diveplane is the wing-like extension on the endplate which is used to enhance downforce on the front axle and to manage the wake around the front tyre by creating tip vortices which accelerate the outwash of air around the tyre.

The dive planes also separate the air which is going over the top of the tyre from that going around the side.

Balancing these two flows will give a cleaner airflow down the sides of the car. They also bring a measure of stability in joining up how the airflow behaves in the transition from straight line to cornering modes as the main wing flaps rotate.

The new dive planes were back-to-back tested in FP1 and FP2. The old ones were used exclusively in FP3 on both cars, when Verstappen was dissatisfied with the car’s balance.

For FP3 and the rest of the event, the new dive planes were fitted to both cars.

Red Bull’s explanation of the new components in the FIA declaration said, “In order to better protect the flow stability of the front wing endplate footplate as well as surfaces downstream, the dive-plane has been lowered and reduced in chord length.”

It’s probably not coincidental that the new dive planes were introduced in the wake of recent tweaks to the sidepod.

The taller, thinner sidepods introduced in Baku were modified for Mexico, with the pre-Baku curved lower section grafted onto the new pods to block off the previous channel along the floor edges.

These were also used in Sao Paulo and Vegas, but a clue into what may have been causing the team problems with the Baku-spec pods was revealed by the insertion of cones within the structure of the lower sections.

These are believed to be to restrict deformation under load.

Getting the required airflow consistency between endplates and sidepods has clearly been a complex matter, but the progress is quite evident in the car’s form.
