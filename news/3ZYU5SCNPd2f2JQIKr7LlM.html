«Играл» ли Рассел «в игры разума с самим собой» в Лас-Вегасе?

In this week’s episode of the F1 Nation podcast, Jolyon Palmer and Alex Jacques join host Tom Clarkson to discuss the latest dramas involving George Russell and Max Verstappen.

George Russell and Max Verstappen were the talk of the town after last weekend’s Las Vegas Grand Prix, with the pair’s long-standing rivalry being renewed via a sequence of on- and off-track moments.

Tensions between the Mercedes and Red Bull drivers – who have intermittently clashed since the 2023 season – flared once more at the Las Vegas Strip Circuit, initially after they banged wheels during final practice.

With Verstappen subsequently being warned by the stewards for driving erratically, Russell took the opportunity to criticise his rival in the post-Qualifying media pen, commenting that “he does this week in, week out, with many drivers”.

“Everybody accepts it, and I am not just going to accept somebody to screw me over like this, and walk around like they are bigger and better than everyone else,” Russell added. “Somebody has got to stand up to that guy.”

Verstappen and Russell went on to share the front row of the grid for the Grand Prix itself, with more battling taking place across an action-packed race before the Briton slid into the barriers on the final lap and the Dutchman secured victory.

Speaking about Russell after the race, Verstappen said: “The thing is, he speaks a lot next to the track, and honestly for me it doesn’t really bother me, because he’s so far below my level that it doesn’t really make any sense for me anyway to try and challenge that.

“And today, karma is a b. It was honestly fantastic when I looked in my mirror and I saw what was happening [with Russell crashing], so I was like, ‘Fantastic – my day is only getting better’. Of course, when I then crossed the line in first, my day was complete.”

In the latest episode of the F1 Nation podcast, sponsored by AWS – Global AI Partner of Formula 1, ex-F1 driver Jolyon Palmer and F1 TV commentator Alex Jacques joined pod host Tom Clarkson to dissect the dramatic turn of events.

While describing Verstappen’s performance as a “demonstration”, and arguing that the four-time World Champion was “a step ahead of everyone in the field”, Palmer pointed to a moment before the race had even started that set the scene for Russell.

“I mean, George talks of intimidation or drivers being bullied, and I felt like there was times when Max was almost… he was intimidating or bullying, but in a fair way, in a race craft way,” he began. “He would just fire a nose in there and it would look like it spooked George to open up the steering.

“The other thing I thought was you had the formation lap, where George goes ahead for half a lap, which was a bizarre situation. It was obviously George trying to play a bit of mind games, and I just thought, almost he’s playing mind games with himself, because I really don’t know how much Max engages in this.

“That’s a fascinating quote and a pretty punchy one from Max [about his level over Russell]. His level is extremely high, and unfortunately for George, he’s off the back of the most difficult season of his career. So, that will be a hard one for him to swallow when he reads it too.”

Jacques supported Palmer’s analysis, adding: “George doesn’t need to be doing that [on the formation lap]. He doesn’t need to be trying to get in [on] the mind games. He doesn’t need to be dropping very, very easy headlines about a practice incident.

“He needs to learn as much as possible, improve the performance, improve the understanding of where he’s been lacking up against his team mate, finish the season strong. It doesn’t really matter about the results; it matters about the performance.

“There’s no way Max gives that quote if George doesn’t go to the [media] pen after Qualifying and say that. So, he doesn’t need to commit as many headlines as he does. Then you’ve got a two-footed tackle at the end of a Grand Prix, where Max has shown all of his race craft, he’s taken the win, George has hit the barriers, and then this on top.”

Verstappen was heard referencing Russell’s incident – from which he recovered to P9 – over the radio just moments after it happened (“Haha, well done!”), before doubling down with his aforementioned comments in the post-race press conference.

“He [Verstappen] said it in the race, didn’t he?” former Renault racer Palmer added. “I think he was looking at the big screen when George went off, or he’s looking in his mirrors or something, because there was a pointed comment when George went off, so it’s obviously in his head.

“That was the enthralling part of the Grand Prix, that it’s those two there. Rivalries are great in sport, and I admire George as well for standing firm with what he believes and speaking his mind – I think that’s great for the sport.

“It’s just going to hurt more when they’re lining up on the front row together, the formation lap goes like that, and we’re all talking up the start… he doesn’t get the start, and then the race is one whole Grand Prix where he’s just on the end of a stick, isn’t he?”
