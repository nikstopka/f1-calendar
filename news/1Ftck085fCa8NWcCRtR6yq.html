Что посмотреть на Гран-при США

From the first match point in the championship battle to Max Verstappen chasing a hat-trick, and from Ferrari’s threat at the front to track limits that could keep the drivers on their toes, here are five key things to look out for during what is often an exciting strategic race at Circuit of The Americas…

1. Antonelli’s championship hopes

If you’ve been following any of the United States Grand Prix weekend so far, you’ll have done well to avoid the fact that Kimi Antonelli could become World Champion for the first time here in Austin.

The equation is simple: Antonelli needs to win the race to have any chance of becoming champion on Sunday. Fail to do that, and he will have to wait until Mexico City at least.

By securing pole position, Antonelli has given himself the best possible chance. But even if he does convert that into victory, he needs two other results to go his way. One is for Lewis Hamilton to be no higher than fifth at the flag, but the other is for his Mercedes team mate George Russell to be no higher than ninth.

Given the relative performance levels shown so far at Circuit of The Americas, it feels like a long shot for it to all come together for Antonelli, but Hamilton is starting from fifth so needs to move forward, and with Russell in sixth there is certainly some jeopardy being a bit further back into Turn 1.

Either way, the pressure is off with such a big lead and five further rounds still to go after this one. But if the Italian is still in a position to win the race in the closing stages, then the door will be open for him to celebrate becoming the youngest World Champion in Formula 1 history at the first opportunity.

2. Verstappen going for three in a row

Arriving in Austin, the likelihood of Kimi Antonelli securing the title appeared low, in part due to the form showed by Red Bull and Max Verstappen in recent races.

Verstappen came close to winning in Baku, before taking his first pole position and first victory of the season at the Bahrain Grand Prix in Malaysia, and then followed that by repeating the feat in Singapore.

The four-time World Champion was very much in the mix for pole position here in Austin too, but wasn’t happy with the balance of his car during Q3, and ended up having to settle for second place.

Still, starting from the front row means Verstappen is a threat off the line on Sunday – and with nothing to lose as he is not in the championship picture.

Antonelli perhaps will be a little more cautious in wheel-to-wheel combat given the advantage he holds, and the Red Bull has been strong in race trim regardless.

The title might not be on Verstappen’s mind, but individual race victories are, and a win could well see him move up to third – or even second – in the drivers’ standings.

3. Ferrari in the frame

The final runs of Qualifying saw a surprising lack of improvements among the frontrunners, but that still doesn’t detract from how close it has looked between the top four teams so far this weekend.

The gap between the top five cars in Qualifying was just 0.276s – and that includes Kimi Antonelli, both Red Bull drivers, and both Ferraris.

Ferrari led the way in all three practice sessions but could not convert that into pole on Saturday, with Charles Leclerc lining up fourth on the grid, and Lewis Hamilton in fifth. Like Singapore, both drivers are clearly competitive, and strategy could be a key factor in any chance of victory.

The first hurdle will be a clean first lap, however, and that will be the most obvious opportunity to try and make forward progress, with Isack Hadjar – starting an impressive third – perhaps the biggest obstacle to being able to fight for the win.

If Hadjar proves quick enough to keep the Ferraris at bay in the opening stint at least, then that hurts their chances of using strategy to challenge the top two.

4. Strategies converging late on

One reason strategy is often key in Austin is this track regularly provides two different approaches that are closely matched. A one-stop race has been able to win the race in the past, but two stops have also been a viable option, particularly when there was a step in tyre compounds last year.

Without that step this year, it’s likely a one-stop will be targeted, but degradation was higher than expected on Friday, and making an extra pit stop is certainly not off the table. Anyone going for two stops will likely then be pushing in the final stages of the race to close the gap on those who stayed out.

Trying an alternate strategy could be on the cards for some of the drivers who have grid penalties this weekend, with Gabriel Bortoleto in particular showing speed to challenge the top 10 throughout the running so far but starting at the back after taking multiple power unit components.

He’ll be joined on the back row by Franco Colapinto – who also has a grid penalty – while Arvid Lindblad lines up 14th after Qualifying ninth but taking a five-place grid drop.

5. Track limits

If there has been one relatively consistent weakness for Kimi Antonelli during his short time in Formula 1 so far, it appears to be related to be related to track limits.

Antonelli was on his final warning for exceeding track limits last time out in Singapore, and has picked up time penalties in the past, with a post-race penalty coming his way in Barcelona earlier this year.

It would be unfair to single him out for infringing this weekend, though, as multiple drivers have seen lap times deleted for not staying within track limits at Circuit of The Americas throughout the sessions so far. Both Lando Norris and Ollie Bearman missed out on better starting positions as a result of being penalised.

Turn 19 is a particularly notable one, with the off-camber nature of that corner often seeing drivers run wide, but the final Turn 20, plus both Turn 9 and Turn 12 in the middle of the lap have also caught out a number of those on the grid.

Three occasions of exceeding track limits in a race leads to a black-and-white warning flag, and then a fourth results in a five-second time penalty.

Charles Leclerc explained the wind direction can play a part in making certain corners particularly tricky, and there’s every chance that finishing positions could be impacted by drivers seeing extra seconds added to their race time.
