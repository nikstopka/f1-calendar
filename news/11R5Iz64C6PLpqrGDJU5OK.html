«Такого я еще никогда не видел», — Верстаппен о победе в Лас-Вегасе

Max Verstappen held on for victory in the Las Vegas Grand Prix after a frenetic race which produced slipstream pack racing.

Max Verstappen believes his victory in the Las Vegas Grand Prix was "something that I've never seen before in Formula 1", with the race featuring more than 30 lead changes after a slipstream thriller.

The Red Bull driver led the majority of the 50-lap race from pole position, but was forced to re-pass for the position on multiple occasions as the slipstream and energy deployment meant breaking away from the pack was impossible.

Verstappen faced pressure throughout, notably from the Mercedes of George Russell, but the Briton ran deep at Turn 5 in the closing stages when trying to overtake team mate Kimi Antonelli.

Antonelli looked on course to challenge Verstappen in a showdown to the line, the latter having specifically asked in advance over the radio about deployment for the final lap as the pair crossed the line side-by-side several times.

But inexplicably the new World Champion dropped behind the McLarens of Lando Norris and Oscar Piastri heading onto the final lap, allowing Verstappen to win by 0.6s from Norris as the top five were covered by just three seconds.

"Crazy. Really, really surreal and something that I've never seen before in Formula 1 myself," said Verstappen when asked about the number of lead changes.

"I think in the beginning we had the deployment immediately correct, but of course people are learning and watching what you're doing.

"At one point, I couldn't get back into the lead before the start-finish line, so that's why I asked the question. But I think we had it set up well, and it was just a crazy amount of fighting.

"The slipstream was so big that it was just impossible to drive off, and it was crazy how many people were following."

The victory was Verstappen's third on the streets of Las Vegas and his fourth during the 2026 season, all of which have come across the last six Grands Prix as Red Bull have hit their stride in the latter part of the season.

The result means Verstappen has solidified his P2 position in the drivers' standings, now sitting 37 points clear of Ferrari's Lewis Hamilton, with Russell three points further adrift.

"It didn't start off well at the beginning of the year, but now it's definitely turning around," said Verstappen.

"It's just great to see for everyone as well. Everyone is working really hard to still get these kinds of performances this year and, of course, still learning for next year. So, yeah, very happy with such a crazy race to come out on top."
