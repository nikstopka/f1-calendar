Что сказали команды – день спринта и квалификация в Сингапуре

Пилоты и команды рассказывают обо всех событиях спринта и квалификации на трассе Marina Bay Street перед Гран-при Сингапура.

Ред Булл

Ферстаппен был сбит Расселом в начале спринта, но вернул лидерство, когда его соперник вылетел из строя. С этого момента на него никогда не оказывалось реальное давление сзади, и голландец одержал свою первую победу в Сингапуре. Это была хорошая новость для команды, плохая заключалась в том, что Хаджар был вынужден досрочно уйти в отставку из-за очевидной проблемы с силовым агрегатом.

Голландец продолжил свои сильные выходные с поулом на Гран-при, поскольку он нацелился на то, чтобы полностью взять поул и одержать победу в эти выходные. Но опять же, в квалификации ситуация была неоднозначной: Хаджар боролся с проблемами надежности в первом квартале. Он остановился на трассе, снова двинулся с места, вошел, снова вышел – все безрезультатно: француз не смог установить время круга и выбыл, как только его спросили.

Мы набирали обороты в последних нескольких гонках, поэтому здорово приехать на трассу, где мы боролись в прошлом, и быть конкурентоспособными. Квалификация была очень близкой на протяжении всей гонки, но мы хорошо настроились и сумели выйти первыми, когда она была До сих пор мы все делали правильно, но самым важным завтра будет чистый старт, так что, надеюсь, мы сможем это сделать и завершить уик-энд, который был для нас действительно сильным».

Как только я вышел, я не смог пройти через Я очень хотел проехать круг, поэтому мы вернули машину и снова выехали, чтобы попробовать, но мы просто не смогли. Феррари

Хэмилтон провел невероятный старт в спринте, поднявшись с шестого места на третье, а затем поднявшись на второе место, опередив старого врага Ферстаппена. Хотя он не смог обойти голландца, он все же уронил своего товарища по команде, когда рывок продолжался, двое передних игроков были в своем классе. Леклер бежал третьим, но изо всех сил старался сохранить жизнь в своих шинах, и ему повезло, что он не попал под давление со стороны McLarens, которые были больше заинтересованы в борьбе друг с другом.

Сильная форма Ferrari продолжилась и в квалификации, где Хэмилтон выглядел лучшим игроком как в первом, так и во втором квартале. Но перед третьим сегментом он признался, что внес некоторые изменения в машину, и на трассе внезапно получил несколько сбоев и, таким образом, немного потерял темп. Последние круги Леклера были немного лучше, но в этом не было ничего особенного – в итоге разница между дуэтом составляла 0,069 секунды.

Мерседес

Но его первый круг пошел боком, когда он вылетел из последнего поворота на скользкий бордюр, потерял заднюю часть и врезался в стену. «Завтра давайте посмотрим, что мы можем сделать и какого прогресса мы можем достичь. За одну ночь можно многому научиться».

Андреа Стелла, руководитель команды: «Это был очень долгий, утомительный и сложный день, и дела определенно пошли не так, как нам хотелось. Результат квалификации отражает наш текущий уровень, на котором мы колеблемся между третьим и четвертым местом по производительности. На этой трассе, очевидно, Red Bull и Ferrari опережают нас, и мы боремся с Mercedes. Мы должны продолжать работать над улучшением MCL40, и у нас еще есть несколько разработок, которые уже завершены. в течение последних нескольких недель это будет постепенно вводиться, начиная с раунда в Остине.

«Однако завтра мы должны постараться максимально использовать любые возможности, которые могут возникнуть на такой трассе, но мы должны быть реалистами и признать, что финиша на подиуме, безусловно, будет нелегко добиться.

«Что касается того, что произошло на последнем круге спринта, то очень просто: недопустимо, чтобы два McLaren когда-либо встретились на трассе. Это фундаментальный принцип того, как мы ездим в гонках, и оба гонщика очень хорошо это понимают.

«Как всегда, мы обсуждаем эти инциденты как команда конструктивным и продуктивным образом, поскольку всегда есть чему поучиться и двигаться вперед, чтобы стать сильнее. Это не те разговоры, которые нам нужно вести публично, но мы напомнили нашим водителям, что интересы команды всегда должны быть на первом месте, чего сегодня не произошло.

«Я хотел бы подчеркнуть работу, проделанную командой по устранению повреждений, нанесенных столкновением двух гонщиков в спринте. Команда в гараже под руководством Чарли Хупера проделала исключительную работу, гарантируя, что Лэндо и Оскар смогут максимально использовать свои шансы в квалификации на машине в идеальном рабочем состоянии. Это командный дух, который характеризует команду McLaren Mastercard Formula 1.

«За пределами Сингапура я хотел бы поздравить участника программы развития пилотов McLaren Дриса Ван Лангендонка с его впечатляющей победой в чемпионате Великобритании в Формуле 4, выиграв соревнование в год его новичка».

Гоночные Быки

Лоусон набрал хорошие очки в спринте, сумев избежать неприятностей. Однако на последнем круге для новозеландца это было непросто, поскольку ему только что удалось избежать вращающихся McLarens. Что касается Линдблада, то с самого начала у него была своя собственная машина безопасности. Затем он ненадолго проехал по трассе не в ту сторону, чтобы добраться до зоны разворота и развернуться, и был вызван к стюардам для объяснений.

Они решили не предпринимать дальнейших действий, и это было хорошо, поскольку означало, что Линдблад мог претендовать на это по заслугам. После своего вчерашнего досрочного ухода он гораздо лучше справился со своей второй попыткой попасть в топ-10, присоединившись к своему товарищу по команде.

Арвид Линдблад, спринт: 13-е, квалификация: 10-е, 1:32.781

«Сегодня у нас был приличный результат, особенно после разочаровывающего результата в спринте. Это была немного необычная квалификация, но во втором квартале я чувствовал себя хорошо и смог немного набрать скорость от машины. По мере того, как мы продвигались к третьему кварталу, у меня были проблемы со сцеплением, и мне было трудно собрать все воедино на последнем круге. Нам нужно проанализировать данные и понять, почему это произошло. Тем не менее, я рад быть в первой десятке и в хорошей позиции перед завтрашним днем».

Лиам Лоусон, спринт: 5-е, квалификация: 9-е, 1:32.369

"Overall, it's been a positive day. After finishing P5 in the Sprint race, it was great to have both cars in Q3, however finishing P9 means I'm starting on the tougher side of the grid tomorrow. It's frustrating as we've been very strong this weekend, however I pushed a bit too hard in the final lap, so didn't maximise the potential of the car. That being said, we have good pace, so I'm feeling hopeful for the race. It's been great to see support from New Zealand here in Singapore, so we hope to give the fans a good race and bring home double points."

Mattia Spini, head of Trackside Engineering

"A very eventful day, as is often the case on the Saturday of a Sprint weekend. The rain arrived just before the formation lap, with the entire field opting for Intermediate tyres. Liam drove a very strong Sprint, gaining some valuable points for the team. He managed his tyres well and adapted to the balance changes typical of a drying track. Arvid found it more difficult to extract the required pace as conditions evolved, but gained useful experience in a particularly challenging session.

“A few hours later, we were back in qualifying mode. It was fantastic to see both cars reach Q3 again, confirming that the car was working well, and both drivers did an excellent job. Unfortunately, we had only one new set of Soft tyres available for each car in Q3, which limited our opportunities to improve on our Q2 lap times. We will learn from this and turn our attention to the race, where we start ninth and tenth; getting the start and the opening lap right will be paramount.”

Alpine

Colapinto was one of those to retire early on in the Sprint, with what looked a mechanical issue that stopped his car on track – the Argentinian not even able to coast into a run off area. That left Gasly to fly the flag for the team, and he came close to points. The Frenchman was running in the top eight after a good start, but lost out to Bearman at the Safety Car restart. He lost out to Hulkenberg and Ocon too, but was able to fight back past his countryman – but he finished just outside the points.

Gasly made it all the way to Q3 and out-qualified both Racing Bulls cars in what was a strong one-lap performance, but Colapinto could not join him in the top 10 after clipping the wall hard in Q2 and thus exiting at the second time of asking.

Pierre Gasly, Sprint: 9th, Qualifying: 8th, 1:32.225

“We definitely have mixed feelings after today. The Sprint Race was tough for us and we struggled for power on the straights throughout the race, which we quickly reviewed to understand for next time. In Qualifying we really did a good job, I was very happy with that, and it was probably one of my best Q3 performances of the season so far. It is a very unforgiving track and it has generally been quite a tricky weekend so far. You have to really build up to it and grow in confidence lap after lap. I pushed the limits further and further and it all came together in the end and I was happy with the lap, so it worked out very well for us. I took a lot of risks on that final run, really on the edge, touched a few walls, but that's what we needed to get the Racing Bulls. Tomorrow is a bit of an unknown for everyone without much practice time on high fuel. I see it as a good opportunity, I am looking forward to that and we have put ourselves in a good position, P7 on the grid with the penalty for George [Russell], which means we have to capitalise and score good points.”

Franco Colapinto, Sprint: DNF, Qualifying: 16th, 1:33.787

“It has been a bit of a frustrating day, not only with not getting the most out of Qualifying but also stopping on track in the Sprint earlier in the day with an issue with the car. In the Sprint, I just lost power, and everything shut down, which I know we're looking into further as a team. We made some changes and fixes between the sessions to parts already in the pool and got back out on track. I felt in a good place in Q1 and was able to push and put a good lap time on the board. After that, the car progressively felt more difficult and there was a strange balance shift where I lacked rear grip, particularly on my last lap in Q2. I'm a bit lost as to why that was and it's something I'm sure we'll look into. I made contact with the wall in the final sector of the lap, but I was already quite a bit down on my previous laps, so it did not really impact where I ended up. We will try our best tomorrow to move forward and score points if we can. We know anything is possible here with the weather and some cars not finishing, so let's see where we end up.”

“A bittersweet day in the end for us but, importantly, we concluded it on a positive note with Pierre's excellent Qualifying performance. It means he starts P7 on the grid for tomorrow's race and that gives us an excellent opportunity to collect a handful of points which is becoming increasingly important for us in our Championship battle. The Sprint Race did not go to plan; Pierre had power issues throughout the race and that meant he had no real tools to take the fight to those ahead, who, eventually scored valuable points. Franco stopped on track with a suspected Energy Store issue, which we identified and replaced ahead of Qualifying. He struggled more with car balance and rear grip in Qualifying, which we are working through to understand and improve. It is important we target a smooth race tomorrow, rely on some of our strengths from recent Sundays and aim to put ourselves in a position to score as many points as possible.”

Audi

Bortoleto was an innocent bystander at the Safety Car restart in the Sprint when Albon hit him from behind, forcing the Brazilian into retirement. Hulkenberg though was in the thick of the action elsewhere. He pulled off an elbows-out move on Gasly for P9 and then ran Bearman ahead close until late on. The German inherited two places on the last lap thanks to the McLaren incident, which bagged Audi two points.

Bortoleto had a bit of a snap on his flying lap in Q2 and wound up down in P15, while Hulkenberg narrowly missed out on Q3. The German will start in the top 10 though when Russell’s grid penalty is applied.

Nico Hulkenberg, Sprint: 7th, Qualifying: 11th, 1:32.784

"It was a positive Saturday overall. Picking up a couple of points in the Sprint was a nice bonus and we'll certainly take that. Qualifying was tidy as well. We tried a few things with the setup and threw everything at it in Q2, but in the end we were just missing that little bit of pace to make Q3.

"Starting from P10 [due to George Russell’s penalty] isn't a bad place to be around Marina Bay. We'll look through everything tonight, and hopefully we can put ourselves in the fight for points tomorrow."

Gabriel Bortoleto, Sprint: DNF, Qualifying: 15th, 1:33.658

“It was a tough Saturday for me. This morning in the Sprint, everyone was bunched up in the last sector after the Safety Car restart, and I got hit from behind. There was nothing I could really do: the damage was very marginal, but unfortunately it was enough to make continuing impossible. It’s a shame because a race like this is full of opportunities, and maybe points were on the table today.

“Qualifying wasn’t great either. I made a mistake on my final lap and went straight in Turn 14. I didn’t feel like I had very good grip throughout the session, and I just didn’t manage to put the lap together. Until that point, it was looking quite good, but margins are small and mistakes are costly. Tomorrow, starting from P14 is going to be tough because this is a track where overtaking is very limited, but I’ll try my best and see what progress we can make.”

“I think it was a pretty good session overall, certainly for Nico, who drove very well. He had a clean and tidy run through qualifying and extracted the maximum performance from the car that we had available tonight.

“On Gabi's side, we were a little bit off the pace for reasons we still need to understand. It is certainly not down to the driver, but we need to get to the bottom of why there was a performance difference between the cars throughout the lap, something we haven't seen at any point this season. We will be putting our heads together to understand that.

“With Russell's grid penalty, Nico will move up to P10, while Gabi will start from P14. I think those are good positions from which to fight for points tomorrow.”

Haas

Bearman enjoyed himself in the wet conditions in Singapore, climbing up the order after a good start and picking off Gasly with a bold move to get into the points. He managed to hold P8 for much of the Sprint too, before scoring even more points when he made his way past Piastri and Norris. As for Ocon, he also got Gasly, but then lost out to his countryman late on and finished a still encouraging P10.

The pair improved over one lap from yesterday, with both drivers making it to Q2. Although they did not have the pace for Q3, once Russell’s grid penalty is applied, Haas look like they could be in the mix for points tomorrow.

Oliver Bearman, Sprint: 6th, Qualifying: 12th, 1:32.908

“It was a really good Sprint, the car was mega, super fast, and I think we were flying out there – I’m proud of the team. To score points, it means that we had to be a bit lucky in terms of result, but we also executed well. I’m proud of my performance, as everything came together today. When it does, we score points, and it’s a good reminder for everyone in the team that we have to keep being there or thereabouts, as if we’re not quick enough to score points on merit, there’s always a bit of opportunity that things can happen.

“It was very close later in qualifying, but it was a bit more competitive than yesterday, or at least that was the feeling. I was happy although Q2 wasn’t as clean as Q1. I spent a long time in the garage after the end of Q1 and the tyres didn’t feel great on the first run, so I was a bit behind going into the second run, which definitely cost a bit of lap time, and then I got in traffic in sector three. I don't think that was going to add up to three tenths, but the fact that we were genuinely fighting to get into Q3 is a good thing. There will be opportunities, I’m sure, as tomorrow it’s a very long night, so we’ll keep our heads up to score more points.”

Esteban Ocon, Sprint: 10th, Qualifying: 13th, 1:33.323

“We had a mega start in the Sprint and a mega first couple of laps, we moved forward up the grid. We tried to keep the momentum going, but once the track dried up, everyone was used to the conditions, and we weren’t quick enough. I think we did the maximum of what we could on our side of the garage for qualifying. We made good progress from yesterday with the set-up of the car, but we've lacked grip since the start of the weekend. There are some things on Ollie’s car that would’ve helped us as well, but it’s very positive that it’s working out well for the team. We progressed well, and I tried to push harder on the last run, but the car couldn’t take it – unfortunately, and we couldn’t finish the lap properly. Getting close to the points in the Sprint after starting P16 is hugely positive. We don’t know if it’s going to rain tomorrow, but we’ll try our best for another decent start.”

“It was a decent Saturday, as in the Sprint we weren’t really expecting to score points. Conditions were wet, but that was actually good as we made such a mess of it in Sepang that we really wanted to put those mistakes behind us and make amends. On the face of it, it looked easy as everyone was on inters and no one made a stop, but what I’m happy about is the communication and clarity going on in the background. It wasn’t there last weekend, but was today, so as a team we made the right decisions and stayed calm. For Ollie to finish P6 and score three points is great, as his driving was fantastic, and his overtakes were amazing. In qualifying, we wanted a strong showing but were disappointed with P12 and P13, which again is a good thing. We left some performance out there but with Russell’s penalty, we start P11 and P12, which is a good place to execute a good race to score points. Tomorrow, from this position, we have to be targeting another points finish.”

Williams

Albon braked too late at the restart and hit the back of Bortoleto’s Audi, before being hit himself by Bottas. That left him with car damage and although he pitted for a new front wing, the team eventually opted to retire the car. Sainz started from the pit lane and rose to 12th at the flag, one of the drivers to keep completely out of trouble.

In Qualifying, Albon could not extract much pace from the car and will start down the order. Sainz made Q2 but found traffic in the form of Hulkenberg which did not aid his cause.

Alex Albon, Sprint: DNF, Qualifying: 18th, 1:34.289 "I was relatively happy with my lap, but it wasn't enough for Q2 today. We had to make several changes to the car following the incident in the Sprint, so thanks to the team for getting the car ready for qualifying. It is a long race tomorrow so you never know what might happen. Tyre degradation is looking okay currently, so will be interesting to see how strategy develops across the grid, especially knowing that overtaking is tricky here. Regardless, we'll give it everything in front of the fans tomorrow."

Carlos Sainz, Sprint: 12th, Qualifying: 14th, 1:33.526 "We did some great laps in Q1 - I was feeling confident and the performance was looking solid, so it’s disappointing that we didn’t nail the execution in Q2. We waited too long in the garage and everything got messy from there, as we rushed to the line and got impeded by an Audi in sector 1 on my final push, so hopefully we’ll start tomorrow from P13 and on the clean side. Although overtaking here is extremely difficult, we will do our best to bring home a good result."

James Vowles, Team Principal "A better showing in qualifying than we had yesterday and more in line with where we expected the pace to be in what is a very tight midfield. Carlos did good laps. We missed out on the very last lap by intentionally pushing late to maximise track evolution. One of the systems indicated that he had crossed the line after the flag but in reality he hadn’t and we need to make sure we tighten up that process and keep pushing until such time as we are confident.

"With Alex, it took a large amount of work following the accident in the Sprint and he wasn’t quite as comfortable with the car, but the team in the garage did a great job to get the car ready. We will have a look overnight and see what changes we need to do so both drivers can come back stronger tomorrow. There are plenty of opportunities, we saw that in the Sprint and we just need to be there to pick up the points when it matters."

Aston Martin

Alonso managed to finish the Sprint but was unable to score, despite the large number of retirements. One of those was Stroll, who collided with Perez early on and parked up in a run off area after hitting the barriers. The stewards had a look and opted not to apply a penalty.

Stroll had another moment in Q1, heading into the run off area after missing his braking point. Neither driver made it into Q2 although Alonso got close, exiting in 17th place.

Fernando Alonso, Sprint: 11th, Qualifying: 17th, 1:34.223

“Today in the Sprint, we got the most out of the car and took advantage of other cars’ retirements to finish P11. Unfortunately, Qualifying was more of the same as yesterday’s Sprint Qualifying, and we could only manage P17. My final run was clean, and everything was well managed by the team, but the pace just wasn’t there again. As a result of that, we have taken the decision to replace the PU overnight.”

Lance Stroll, Sprint: DNF, Qualifying: 19th, 1:34.946

“It’s not our weekend so far, we’re not performing here as expected. The car didn’t feel great today - it’s a bumpy track and these tracks are tough for our car. We didn’t manage to capitalize on any opportunities during the Sprint as I had to retire the car early after making contact with another car. In Qualifying, we hit traffic during my final push lap, and there was some confusion about Bottas in front of me aborting his push lap, where we lost a lot of time.”

“A difficult day for us here in Singapore. We had mixed fortunes in the Sprint where Lance made contact with Perez which put him out early, whilst Fernando made up four places to finish in P11. In tonight's Qualifying we did not have the pace to make it beyond Q1. We will change Fernando's PU based on his feedback. Tomorrow will be a long race where everything can happen, we will have to prepare well and be ready to take advantage of any opportunities that come our way.”

Cadillac

It was not to be for Cadillac in the last Sprint of the year. Perez was the first to retire after contract with Stroll’s Aston Martin. He limped back to the pits, but had to retire the car. Then Bottas hit the back of Albon’s Williams at the restart, after the Thai driver had hit the car in front. That led to Bottas also retiring, and left the team with two damaged cars to repair.

Although the team did well to get both cars out at the start of Q1, neither had the pace to fight with the likes of Williams and Aston Martin. Perez took the intra-team honours by over eight tenths.

Valtteri Bottas, Sprint: DNF, Qualifying: 21st, 1:35.984

“It’s been a tough day - I didn’t finish the Sprint and in Qualifying I had a problem with deployment on my last lap, so I didn’t have any power on the straights. I was still pushing, but it felt like I was in charge mode, so I didn’t have a chance to improve. We need to have a look at the procedure and see what happened there. It’s not been straightforward so far, but let’s see what happens tomorrow.”

Sergio Perez, Sprint: DNF, Qualifying: 20th, 1:35.123

“The team did a great job getting the car ready again after the incident in the Sprint. Technically, the car felt a little better today, but the gap to our competitors remains too big. Aston Martin and Williams seem to be stronger than us around this circuit. I got the absolute maximum out of the car and put together one of the strongest laps I’ve ever done here. I’m really pleased with my lap, but unfortunately, even with such a strong performance, it wasn’t enough to gain a single position. We know the areas we need to work on for next year, particularly our low-speed performance and the car’s mechanical behavior.”

“A mixed Sprint Saturday for the team today with a heavy downpour just before the start of the Sprint. We had to retire both cars from the Sprint race and that gave the garage a lot of work to do before Qualifying - so a big thank you to everyone for the hard work. Checo got tangled up with [Lance] Stroll and Valtteri got caught out with the concertina effect behind the Safety Car at the restart.

“Qualifying for tomorrow’s race was a bit more positive. Both drivers had a clean session but Valtteri had a deployment issue on his car which meant he was down on power on his last push lap. We will have a couple of cars starting behind us on the grid tomorrow and on a street circuit we have to be ready to take our chances with our current package.”

Pirelli Dario Marrafuschi, Pirelli Motorsport Director "The conditions faced by the drivers during the Sprint were challenging because rainfall had affected only part of the circuit, effectively leaving half of the track very wet while the other half was already drying. The Intermediate tyres used at the start remained on the cars until the chequered flag and delivered remarkably consistent performance throughout the race distance. Despite the track evolving towards near-slick conditions in the closing stages, degradation remained extremely limited and the graining we observed, particularly on the front axle, had no significant impact on performance. Managing tyre temperatures was a key factor, especially in the final laps, but overall tyre wear remained fully under control.

"The track still featured some damp sections during qualifying, resulting in less than ideal conditions. Teams adopted different approaches when preparing the Soft tyre. Some elected to complete an additional preparation lap to bring the tyres into their optimum operating window, as Mercedes did in Q3, although no significant advantage emerged compared to rivals who opted to push immediately on a flying lap. Once again, it was clear that tyre management is an essential part of the overall package, closely linked to both the characteristics of the car and the driver’s style.

"In our view, the C5 represents the most interesting option for the start of tomorrow’s race, which could be heavily influenced by any Safety Car periods. The higher level of grip offered by the Soft in the opening phase should make it the preferred choice for most drivers, who may look to extend the first stint, aided by the low degradation levels, while waiting for a favourable neutralisation. For the second part of the race, the Medium and Hard offer relatively similar performance, although the C4 could provide an advantage in terms of grip. At present, a two-stop strategy appears unlikely, as it is estimated to be around ten seconds slower than the available one-stop alternatives."
