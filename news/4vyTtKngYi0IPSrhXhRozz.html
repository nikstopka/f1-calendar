Браун о том, почему McLaren с трудом боролся за титул в 2026 году

McLaren CEO Zak Brown has reflected on why the team were unable to defend their titles in 2026 – and the learnings that they are taking forward into next season.

McLaren CEO Zak Brown has given his take on the reasons why the team struggled to challenge for the championship in 2026 – but the American was also keen to avoid an “excuse”, adding that the squad have “just got to do a better job”.

The 2024 season saw the papaya outfit score their first constructors’ title since 1998 – and while Max Verstappen took the drivers’ crown on that occasion, McLaren clinched both championships in 2025 with Lando Norris claiming his maiden triumph.

Amid the introduction of sweeping new technical regulations for the 2026 season, the team were overtaken by rivals including Mercedes and Ferrari as the campaign got underway.

Signs of progress emerged later in the season – with Norris taking back-to-back victories in Hungary and the Netherlands before more recently winning again in Sao Paulo – but McLaren remain in fourth place of the teams’ standings on a 17-point deficit to third-placed Red Bull.

During an appearance on Sky Sports' The F1 Show, Brown was quizzed on whether there was an impact into 2026 from the fact that the team were developing their 2025 car late into that season.

“What’s great is we kind of never talk about it, because we don’t want to have excuses,” Brown responded. “But, reality is you get the least amount of wind tunnel time because the team that won gets it – but we never kind of talk about that.

“We were developing and racing pretty hard last year up to the last race. Some teams went, ‘We’re done for ’25, let’s focus on ’26.’ And then also, not being a works team, when a new technology comes out, there’s a lag of understanding how everything works.

“So if I think about how we know the power unit works today versus Australia, night and day, and the works team – rightfully so – they get a head start on you because they’re the works team.

“We’ve now kind of caught up, maybe not yet to their level of knowledge, but we’ve come a long way. So when you kind of package that all together… but what I never want that to make it sound like is an excuse. We’ve just got to do a better job.”

With this in mind, Brown admitted that there are plenty of learnings that the team will take forward from the MCL40 for their 2027 challenger.

"Weight was a big issue this year for a lot of cars, obviously a shorter wheelbase [results in a] lighter car,” he explained.

"So yeah, I think there's things that… Slow speed corners weren't great for us. We've learned a lot about the power unit this year, and deployment, and all the stuff that we're all still learning.

"I think this season, there's a lot of takeaways and changes we're going to make going into 2027."
