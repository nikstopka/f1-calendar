Генеральный директор Cadillac Таурисс о будущем Herta и расширении Bottas

With Cadillac opting to retain Valtteri Bottas for 2027 rather than promoting Colton Herta, CEO Dan Towriss has explained what the next steps might be for the American driver.

Cadillac CEO Dan Towriss has given an update on Colton Herta’s future after the American missed out on a potential F1 promotion in 2027, following the announcement that Valtteri Bottas will remain with the squad.

While Sergio Perez’s contract extension was confirmed ahead of the Mexico City Grand Prix in October, speculation persisted over whether Bottas would stay in the other seat or if Herta – who has acted as a test driver for the team this year – might be given the opportunity.

Alongside embarking on his debut F2 campaign in 2026, Herta has participated in six Free Practice 1 outings for Cadillac – meaning that he accumulated enough Super Licence points to allow him to race in F1.

However, the American outfit ultimately decided to retain Bottas for 2027, a decision that Towriss reiterated was “right” for them as he spoke to the media on Thursday at the Las Vegas Grand Prix.

When asked if there was ever any doubt over Bottas’ position, Towriss responded: “We did want to take a look at it. I think as we look across the races, while [his pace in] practice and Qualifying was similar to Checo, race pace was down.

“Some of that [involves] things behind the scenes that are out of Valtteri's control. We did want to take a look at that. We were working hard to get Colton Herta ready as well.

“It's important to have an American driver for our team with our team, racing, but ultimately, when we took a look at it, Valtteri's the right guy.

“We want to keep Checo and Valtteri together, their experience, the continuity, what they bring to the team. Ultimately it was the right decision for the team and we were happy to extend Valtteri. He's been absolutely fantastic for us.”

In terms of what this means for Herta’s next steps, Towriss explained that there are “a number of options on the table” for the 26-year-old.

“We've offered him a reserve position,” the American said. “The seat's open for ’28, so there are options on the table for Colton. There are options in other sports as well. I think he's decided on a direction, but we're still in conversations on that and look forward to seeing what Colton wants to do.”

Towriss acknowledged that Herta was “disappointed” by the decision and went on to praise the former IndyCar racer for taking on the challenge of the past year.

“It was definitely a tough decision – a tough discussion with Colton,” he explained. “I think he was disappointed. There was a lot of work, a lot of a build-up [to] go into this. I know Colton was very brave in even taking on this risk, going into F2 and trying to cram all this learning in.

“I think it was obvious it was a very tough season for Colton in F2 this year, but I think the work that he did in FP1s and the TPC [Testing of Previous Cars] setting [was] quite strong and showed that he really is capable of driving this car. But when we look at where the team is right now and what's needed for the team, Valtteri was the best decision going forward.

“But we're going to continue to talk to Colton about the options that are on the table and give it a little bit of time, because I think he is disappointed to not have that opportunity. So much went into this, but he's just a tremendous talent and we're excited about what's ahead for him.”
